# The five bright planets

The points for Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn now accompany the real star catalogue. They use the same sky brightness, atmospheric extinction, horizon/far-land hiding, pixel footprint and exposure as the stars, in the same draw call. They stay steady rather than scintillating. The existing lunar position and illuminated phase remain intact. This is a low-precision visual ephemeris, not telescope pointing or an observation forecast.

`planets.mjs` implements [JPL's approximate elements](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html), its 1800–2050 table, with a Newton Kepler solve and one light-time iteration. Positions are J2000 equatorial; the stars' existing precession and sidereal transform take them into the scene. Earth is the Earth–Moon barycentre approximation, without lunar correction, nutation, aberration or topocentric planetary parallax. Outside the table's date interval the five points are hidden.

Visual magnitudes implement [Mallama and Hilton 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01973), equations 2–4, 6–8 and 10: Mercury's sixth-order curve, Venus's two branches, Mars's mean phase curve, Jupiter's geocentric curve and Saturn's illuminated ring inclination. Mars omits the longitudinal and seasonal albedo correction, which accounts for some reference differences. Saturn's J2000 pole supplies ring inclination. These are original equation implementations; no third-party code, imagery or model is included. The paper and JPL page are cited research references, not redistributed assets.

`bin/fetch_planet_references.py` retrieves airless, geocentric astrometric ICRF positions and V magnitudes from JPL Horizons. `test/fixtures/planet-horizons.json` preserves twenty actual responses across January, April, July and October 2026, their query URLs and retrieval date. `test/coastal-planets.test.mjs` requires less than 0.25° angular separation and 0.2 magnitude error against every reference. Those are bounded sample tolerances, not a precision claim across the full 250-year element interval.

Only five vertices are added to the existing point draw. Directions and magnitudes refresh once per astronomical minute; they do not integrate from frame rate or paused scene time. Pausing motion freezes scintillation and water while the live civil sky retains its existing clock convention. Reduced motion holds all points steady.
