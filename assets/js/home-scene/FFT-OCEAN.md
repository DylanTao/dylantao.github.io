# A spectral ocean, in the lab

The authoring URL `/?scene-lab=1&ocean=fft` replaces the long-wave field with an actual 64 × 64 inverse FFT over a periodic 128 m square. The public scene keeps its existing analytic ocean. `fft-ocean.mjs` is an original implementation of the Fourier height-field construction and Phillips spectrum described in [Jerry Tessendorf's course notes, section 4](https://jtessen.people.clemson.edu/reports/papers_files/coursenotes2002.pdf). The paper is a research reference: no reference code, imagery, shader or third-party model is redistributed.

The initial complex Gaussian coefficients follow a Phillips spectrum, with a fixed 8 m/s reference wind toward local +Z, a short-wave cutoff of 0.8 m, and a factor of 0.25 for the reverse wind half-plane. The finite realization is normalized for approximately 0.13 m RMS height. Hermitian time evolution uses `h(k,t) = h0(k) exp(iωt) + conjugate(h0(-k)) exp(-iωt)`, with deep-water `ω² = g |k|`; an actual radix-two inverse FFT runs across rows and then columns. Zero DC preserves mean sea level. This is a fixed reference sea, not a reconstruction of today's wind or a forecast.

One RGBA Float32 texture carries height and central-difference slopes. CPU and GPU use the same periodic four-texel interpolation, with the same mesh-local origin (the sea is centred 28 m offshore). The water geometry, pixel normals, the swimming sea lions, sandpiper swash-edge search and pelican water contacts consume that shared field. The existing bathymetry, shoreline foam, capillary normals, planar reflection, daylight, animal wakes and water optics remain active. Reflection remains a distorted planar reference, not curved ray tracing. There are no boats in the current scene to validate or silently claim as supported.

The scene's active clock updates the rendered field once per active frame. Paused, reduced-motion, hidden and offscreen scenes retain their existing clock policy. Wildlife's half-second stepping plans and fixed future flight endpoints use a separate bounded cache of at most 24 forecast fields, so asking for a future water contact cannot replace the texture drawn at the present instant. Releasing the environment clears those forecasts and disposes the FFT texture. The default analytic path allocates no FFT texture and compiles no FFT sampling branch.

This is deliberately a first experiment: vertical displacement only, a single periodic tile, no horizontal choppiness, no cascades, no JONSWAP, no depth-dependent dispersion, no breaking-wave solver, no foam transport, and no two-way fluid/animal coupling. The central-difference slope field is a sampled normal approximation, not the exact derivative of a piecewise bilinear height field. Short-wave slope filtering remains the existing analytic treatment; the experiment does not implement a full multiscale spectral BRDF.

`test/coastal-fft.test.mjs` checks inverse-transform reconstruction, periodicity, deterministic heights, zero mean, held-clock reuse and finite normals. `bin/verify_environment.cjs` captures the actual full scene with candidate modules/models and production HTML/CSS at a matched camera, preview minute, viewport and exposure. Captures and full evidence are retained under `.jekyll-cache/visual-qa/environment/`.

The October 23 interleaved local A/B/B/A captures measured analytic/FFT/FFT/analytic median frame intervals of 32/34/31/31 ms and submission times of 16/19/15/15 ms. These are noisy shared-laptop results while other scene workers were active, not an isolated GPU benchmark or a speed claim. The current field's FFT took 0–2 ms in the browser's integer-resolution timing. The 64² RGBA texture uploads 65,536 bytes per active frame (about 3.75 MiB/s at 60 Hz); the forecast cache caps at 1,572,864 bytes. No new ocean geometry, draw call or reflection pass is added. The final release still needs the coordinator's integrated visual/performance checkpoint.

## Still-photo supersampling

`still-photo.mjs` supplies a separate finite photo operation for the authoring lab. It takes 2–16 centred Halton jitter samples of a **frozen** composed view, reads the actual output, decodes sRGB, averages in linear light and encodes a PNG-ready canvas. It restores the exact projection and inverse projection even on failure; a changed view discards the result. A 2.2 megapixel limit bounds allocation. The controller must require paused/reduced motion and a fixed preview minute. This is spatial supersampling performed over several renders of one still, with no history from moving frames. It does not complete motion-vector TAA, disocclusion rejection, animated transparency history or motion-blur reconstruction; those remain unsupported until separately implemented and measured.
