# The clifftop water garden

`bin/coastal_water_garden.py` authors an original recirculating creek and koi pond into the independent `hinterland.blend`, and exports them with `hinterland.glb`. The full hinterland builder calls the same function before its camera-grid/export stage. It never opens or saves the dwelling's source. All geometry and runtime fish are original; no third-party asset, image or model is included.

The pond is centred at world `[-23, 10.0357, 52]`, with a 3.2 m radius and a slightly longer east–west axis. The head basin starts at `[-16, 10.3807, 76]`; six descending reaches feed the pond. The route follows the supported eastern terrace rather than bridging the existing gully. Local ground is subdivided to resolve the 68 cm channel, carved into an actual bed and cleared of only the planting triangles in the water/bank corridor. The house, entry route, mainland boundaries, existing groves and animal habitats remain separate. Camera-grid heights stay conservatively above the lowered ground.

The native report at `artwork/coastal-home/reviews/water-garden.json` raycasts 78 creek samples against the final carved terrain, measuring 0.062–0.471 m water-to-bed separation, plus 64 pond samples at 80% radius, measuring 0.421–0.424 m. Those are sampled support checks, not a continuous fluid or collision proof. The builder fails if the sampled creek intersects the ground or spans more than 0.8 m above it. Native custom vertex data retains original ground elevations for repeatable carving. Existing baked terrain colours are retained; this is not a new full-landscape Cycles light bake.

`water-garden.mjs` gives five original koi tapered bodies, dorsal/paired fins, inset eyes, pigment patches and articulated tails. Their paths, tails and the water's prescribed surface normals share the home's active clock. The fish stay inside the basin. They are authored swimming, not a fluid, animal dynamics or collision solver. The creek is a garden circulation, with no claim that a natural freshwater stream exists here. It adds no second fluid solver.

The onsen's existing native bamboo kakei supplies its new visible drops. `coastal_decor.py:bamboo_spout` defines the tip at world `[3.72, 3.28, 3.62]`. The adapter attaches only the drop, leaving that original fixture intact. Its prescribed fall adds a small zero-mean impulse to the existing height-field solver when it lands. The one-way hand coupling remains. Drop timing, ripples and the solver stop under Pause/reduced motion or when the bath is not stepped; no missed-drop debt accumulates. Tests verify closed-basin volume conservation and disposal.

The far landscape also gains original grove crown silhouettes, meadow/soil variation and warm settlement points beyond the modelled world. Groves ease in over 60 m outside the captured hinterland, and retain the far field's sun/moon light, terrain hiding, haze and reflection path. Lighting is an authored pattern on land, concentrated south and inland; the northern reserve and open water stay dark. It is not a GIS inventory or measured light-pollution model.

`test/coastal-environment.test.mjs` verifies the native support report, house clearance, descending reaches, deterministic shared-clock swimming, held poses, onsen volume and land-only settlement lights. Actual candidate browser frames are captured by `bin/verify_environment.cjs` using source-asset interception against the coordinator's production build; it does not rely on another worker's server. Local PNGs, full frame evidence, errors, held-clock/pose checks and movement comparisons are under `.jekyll-cache/visual-qa/environment/`. The integration coordinator owns the final four-viewport and release checkpoint.
