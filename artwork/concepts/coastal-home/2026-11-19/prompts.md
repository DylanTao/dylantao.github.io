## Speakeasy 01 · Approved architectural concept

Sirui approved the seated interior and the cliff section after rejecting the first built bar and exterior lift tower. The original composition came from his description, the native room survey and a study of the junction between the speaker wall and entry at Equipment Room in Austin. The prompt used no external image input. [Original](speakeasy-01.png) · [Architects’ project record](https://www.rios.com/projects/equipment-room/).

> Use case: stylized-concept. Architectural design study for an original, tiny private hi-fi speakeasy carved into the warm, stratified sandstone of a La Jolla cliff house. One wide landscape image with two architectural views, no writing or labels: a large 75 percent left-hand panel of the interior at a seated human eye 1.2 m above the floor, and a smaller 25 percent right panel of this same elevator seen from the beach. Interior: a real compact fourteen-square-metre chamber, low 2.55 m ceiling, the bedded stone is the ceiling and back wall. The bar's one memorable object is a pair of enormous sculpted honey-walnut exponential speaker horns with dark circular throats flanking a glowing shallow circular inset in the rock over a real record console. Both horn mouths and the low shelving of record spines are built into one carefully made acoustic cabinet. A green soapstone bar with a broad softly doglegged rounded end leads toward a horizontal low slit of peaceful blue Pacific. The counter's chamfer shows mineral veins, below it fine walnut reeds, a continuous dark bronze foot rail. Three or four beautifully made saddle-backed copper-brown leather listening stools on tapered wooden legs. A short rust/moss velvet banquette, a single quiet little lamp on a table, a few pieces of proper cocktail glassware in use, and a subtle pool of honey-colored light at a bronze-lined lift doorway cut directly into the rear stone wall. Warm indirect grazers reveal actual stratification. No generic grid of bottles, no ubiquitous pendant bulbs, no white restaurant walls, no neon, no people. Controlled exposure, textured warm amber rather than orange, sage green and a little blue. Outside view at evening: a sheer continuous light sandstone face over the beach. The cliff itself is the enclosure for the concealed lift. Only one narrow deeply recessed vertical amber-edged window cut into the rock is visible with a glimpse of glass cab inside; the cliff continues above and beside it. A low portal opens from the shaft onto a dry rocky beach shelf. Never show a free-standing lift tower, bridge, scaffolding or rooftop machine room. Original interiors, refined and surprising like a destination American record bar. Calm, welcoming and real, beautifully crafted. A photographic architect's material reference for a realtime Blender build.

## Painted coast 01 · Colour study

The reference input is the site’s own November 17 six-view WebGL contact sheet. The top two panels supplied the native silhouette and waterline. [Original drawing](painted-coast-01.png) · [Reference frame](../../../../docs/images/painted-second-pass-2026-11-17.jpg).

> Use case: stylized-concept. Make an original production colour key for a real-time hand-painted game world based on the supplied contact sheet of Sirui's existing California cliff house. Use ONLY its upper two coast views as the authoritative camera, silhouette, horizon/waterline, the roof and window shapes, tiny shoreline animals if legible. Ignore the lower four interiors and all captions. Keep the same close offshore perspective, composition, uncluttered sloping foreground cliff and scale of the little glazed rooms, with open sky above. One wide colour script DIPTYCH, two equal landscape panels of exactly the same view separated by a very narrow warm-paper gutter, no lettering anywhere. Left panel: late afternoon opaque gouache on warm grey paper; generous shaped strokes across honey sandstone, broad low-chroma violet shadow wedges, grey-cyan sky, a small but unmistakable warm-dark house under an overhang, jade water in the shallows with the white edge of one breaker. Right panel: twilight painted in soft-bodied oil, quiet indigo and muted malachite coast, a last cool salmon rim on the upper rock, one inviting amber window at the heart of the little house; strong darks that keep the rock mass, a few rounded warm reflections carried horizontally by water. Real foreground/middle/distance brush-size variation. Establish a clear value design that survives a thumbnail. Break silhouettes selectively with a dry edge. Scarce dark lines localized to architecture. Strokes follow each rock stratum, pine needles group into a few tapered clusters, foam moves with the shore, broad quiet sky with breathing room. Serene, joyful and inhabited. Do not zoom out, invent a different house, add lenticular clouds or cliffs, turn the whole palette orange, scatter blue glitter, create a printed poster or write labels. This is a designer's target for implementing lighting, directional brush marks, plant geometry and shaders in the actual house. No signature or imitation of a named artist.

## Sandstone 01 · Material study

This study supplies a diffuse surface from which to build a small repeatable stone material. It was generated without an input image. [Original material study](sandstone-01.png).

> PBR material albedo for a native 3D architectural model: one original square edge-to-edge seamless tile, orthographic, straight-on flat capture of warm buff San Diego marine sandstone, as if exposing a freshly hand-cut face inside a La Jolla cliff. Natural gently undulating bedded parallel horizontal sediment layers range from fine grey-gold sandy lines through peach beige and warm raw sienna; several shelves flake naturally, thin bedding laminae slip and pinch out rather than forming uniform waves. The swath represents 1.8 metres wide by 1.8 metres high, with very fine sandy mineral grain, about six broad 12–24cm beds with irregular thinner beds between, occasional rounded inclusions and two hairline joints. Neutral diffuse D65 light over the entire surface, no directional illumination, no large cast shadow, no baked rim lights, no yellow tint from room lights, no oblique perspective, no border, no furniture or scenery, no text or labels. Believable sediment and restrained color with enough local contrast to read close to a visitor. Continue the horizontal laminae through the left/right picture edges. This is a texture to wrap the real cut ceiling and walls of an original computer-generated speakeasy. Not a picture of the room.
