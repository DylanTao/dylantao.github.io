## Rebuild

Run `blender --background --python-exit-code 1 --python bin/build_coastal_home.py` from the repository root. The script writes the editable sources, GLBs, and model-review portraits. Each character source opens on the idle NLA track; choose another track to inspect its clip. The four human variants share bone names; Lizard adds Tail and TailTip. Append `-- --house-only`, `-- --characters-only`, `-- --lizard-only`, or `-- --avatar=ghibli` (any of the five avatar IDs) for a focused rebuild.

The September 12 refinement replaces disconnected body masses with continuous remeshed skin, flowing hair surfaces, fitted clothing, and blended arm/leg weights. Mouths are recessed geometry; Ghibli uses a quieter closed smile. The house has a vaulted stone envelope connected to inland terrain, a cliff descending roughly seven meters to the tide, a sloping crescent beach, and receding headlands. Roof vegetation follows the roof's cutaway visibility. The subsequent Realistic pass replaces the old disconnected relief chips with continuous cliff fluting; illustrated erosion strokes remain a deferred lab experiment.

The installed Microsoft Store launcher denied background execution. The implementation used the official portable Blender 4.5.9 LTS archive from `download.blender.org`, verified against the release SHA-256 list. No Store permissions were changed.

The subsequent Realistic pass adds `bin/coastal_craft.py`: original mesh floorboards, joinery, built-in shelves/books, keyboard keys, ceramic cup, thin curved leaves and stems, cabinet fronts, lanterns, a braided rug, irregular onsen stones, and folded linen. Cliff fluting and erosion are modeled into the continuous surface. Physical-scale procedural material detail, sky/environment light, contact occlusion, shoreline wash, live water reflection, onsen ripples, and soft vapor are authored in the browser. No commercial textures or scenic backdrop images are used.

Three.js r164 postprocessing and Draco loader sources, plus Google's Apache-2.0 Draco decoder, are pinned locally with source hashes in `assets/js/vendor/three-r164/README.md`. The static worker entry point is extracted from that exact loader by `bin/prepare_draco_worker.py`; it works with the existing site CSP. The sea's oblique reflection-camera construction follows the official r164 Water implementation.

Asset creation used Blender's Python API in background mode. Mouse/keyboard control of the native Blender application was unavailable in this Codex session. The `.blend` files are editable geometry and rigs, not generated pictures presented as models. Append `-- --export-only` to read the existing sources and re-export every GLB with Draco compression, restoring all character animation tracks for export.

The full home source is excluded from Jekyll via `artwork/`. Only the optimized browser assets ship with the website.

After a focused room-source change, `bin/bake_coastal_light.py -- --room=sleep` and `bin/optimize_coastal_exports.py -- --room=sleep` rebake/export that room only, retaining the other room records in the review reports. Any of the six room IDs is accepted. Omit that option for a complete house bake/export.

The September 13 habitat refinement adds `bin/coastal_interiors.py`: original curved wall ribbons, rounded alcove returns, an arched print niche, oak battens, rubber tiles, a four-post rack, loaded barbell with collars, padded bench and two-tier dumbbell stand. `bin/coastal_characters.py` rebuilds the four human variants against the supplied character board, with explicit jaw profiles, inset eyes, off-centre hair sweeps and tapered locks, relaxed sleeves, individual fingers and laced canvas shoes. All ten clips use the established bone/contact convention. The Lizard geometry is retained. The same capybara print replaces every avatar-dependent wall portrait; no generated backdrop was introduced.

## October 15: the land around the mainland

Sirui found the left, right and back of the mountain lazy. The retained mainland ended in flat walls at its back (y = -32 m) and at both ends (x = -36 m and 76 m), so from outside, or zoomed out, the land stopped like a cake slice. `bin/build_coastal_hinterland.py` builds what continues from those walls as its own asset, `hinterland.glb`, with its source in `hinterland.blend`; `coastal_hinterland.py` holds the design and `coastal_hinterland_props.py` the planting and fixtures. The build appends the mainland's block, foundation and beach from `coastal-home.blend` into a fresh file and never saves the source. It snaps the new ground to the 225 back-edge and 41 end-edge vertices of the retained top surface (largest offset 1 µm) and continues the mainland's own `top_height`, `cliff_surface` and `beach_point` across each seam, so height and slope match there. The flat walls are now buried.

- Inland, a coastal mesa of stepped marine terraces rises toward a long hill (after Mount Soledad, not to scale). Two canyons cut it, one reaching the beach past each end of the mainland. Their fluted badland walls have ribs standing forward of rounded gullies under an iron-red cap, as at Torrey Pines, and small ravines notch the tall northern bluffs.
- North, the bluffs climb from the mainland's 15 m to about 32 m of pale Torrey sandstone over a long beach, with deep gullies and talus. The gliderport's grassy launch is on top, with a post-and-chain edge fence, two benches and an orange-and-white wind sock on a 6.4 m mast. The sock is its own node, `hinterland_windsock`: its hoop is at the node origin and the sock runs along local +Z, and the manifest's `hinterland.windsock` records its pivot and still pose. `hinterland.mjs` turns it with the coast's wind (`coastWind.vane` in `wind-field.mjs`): it points downwind and hangs or stands out with each gust.
- South, a cove with a wide beach lies at the south canyon's mouth. A headland beyond it holds three sea caves at the waterline in its north face, under a timber viewing deck with a bench and a coin-operated binocular viewer, as at Goldfish Point. The Coast Walk leads to the deck. A second trail runs along the mesa behind the house, round the north canyon's head, to the gliderport. A distant point closes the southern view.
- Sage, chaparral, buckwheat, the washes' riparian shrubs and 156 Torrey pines are low-poly masses in one vertex-coloured canopy material: denser and finer near the house, coarser far away. The ground's vertex colours follow the same cover map. The retained clifftop's own planting stops about 20 m inland, so a sparse transition planting (136 shrubs, in this asset) covers the mainland's bare rear strip; it keeps 2.2 m from every flora vertex, the doorway and its stepping stones. No shrub stands where the ground rises or falls a metre within its reach, so none hangs over a rim.
- Three paragliders and a hang glider soar the ridge lift along the north bluffs (`assets/js/home-scene/gliders.mjs`). Their poses follow the scene clock, so they stop when motion is paused and hold still under reduced motion.
- The shore shader clamps its surf line to the mainland's end values, so every new beach keeps its waterline seaward of that line and no surf floats offshore.

Ambient occlusion is baked into vertex colours with the mainland as an occluder; open ground bakes to 0.95, as on the house. Within 8 m of a seam the shade eases to the retained surface's own baked value. The outside camera's terrain grid grows over the new land as data. `hinterland.cameraGrid` samples the land on the retained 2 m lattice, each node holding the highest ground within 2 m. `hinterland.mjs` merges it into `cameraCollision` when the land loads, and the retained cells win. The asset loads on the first visit outside or to the whole house, like the flora, and stays out of the first scene's bytes. `reviews/hinterland.json` records the build. - Torrey Pines Gliderport: [paragliders over the launch, with its wind sock](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Torrey_Pines_Gliderport_1.jpg) (Ncysea, CC BY-SA 4.0), [the grassy launch field](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Torrey-pines-gliderport.JPG) (Dmadeo, CC BY-SA 3.0) and [the post-and-chain fence at the bluff edge](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Torrey_Pines_Gliderport_2_2017-10-19.jpg) (FASTILY, CC BY-SA 4.0).

## October 15: hair, brows and expressions from Sirui's portraits

Sirui found that the four human figures did not match his 2D portraits (`assets/img/sirui_pic_ghbili.png`, `sirui_pic_south_park.png`, `sirui_pic_simpsons.png`, `sirui_pic_rick_and_morty.png`). In every portrait the hair is shoulder-length and black (very dark brown in the Ghibli one), parted at the crown and full enough to frame the face down past the jaw, with the ends flicking outward. The models' hair was slicked to the skull like a cap, with thin strands at the back. The portraits also have thick dark brows and open expressions.

`bin/coastal_likeness.py` replaces only the head features. The full builder and `bin/refresh_coastal_likeness.py` both call it. It removes the old hair, brows, face skin and mouth parts. It then rebuilds the face with the builder's own recipe, now shared as `coastal_characters.adult_face`, and cuts each style's mouth into it:

- South Park: a large D-shaped mouth with a black interior and four teeth.
- Simpsons: a wide overbite grin with a row of upper teeth.
- Rick and Morty: a wide open grin with scalloped teeth and a tongue, on a broader, rounder jaw. The jaw widens from below the eyes, so the eyes and glasses do not move.
- Ghibli: a gentle open smile with a tongue, on a slightly softer jaw.

The hair is one closed shell over the skull. It is thickest on top and leaves an arched forehead under the side part, with more volume on the side the hair sweeps toward. Above each ear it sweeps down from the forehead and stops just above the ear and the glasses arm. Behind the ear it falls in a curtain to about the jaw, flaring into pointed locks that flick outward. The brows are thick swept bars set on the forehead above each glasses ring.

Every new vertex is skinned fully to Head, so the hair moves with the head in all fourteen clips; there is no secondary motion. The rig, skin weights, body, hands, feet, neck, glasses, eyes, eyelids and Blink morphs are unchanged, and so is every authored key. In the exported GLBs the animation samples and inverse bind matrices are byte-identical. The back of the hair stays inside the old hair's rearmost point, so the sleep clip still rests the head where it did. Near the neck the curtain's inner surface keeps 2.2 cm clear, so the deepest forward head pitch (reading) cannot press hair into it. The Lizard has no 2D reference and is unchanged.

`reviews/character-likeness.json` records bytes and triangles. GLB sizes before and after are 718,608 to 716,452 (South Park), 671,740 to 669,956 (Simpsons), 854,348 to 861,208 (Ghibli) and 655,740 to 663,812 (Rick and Morty). `bin/review_coastal_likeness.py` writes `reviews/character-likeness-review.json`, which records the following against `f403dfead`:

- Everything outside the replaced features is exactly retained.
- No hair is inside the ears, glasses rims or eyelids.
- Only a few glasses-arm vertices tuck into the hair at the ear.
- No hair is inside the body in any clip except the sleep clip and South Park's pull-up. Both had the same contact before: the sleep clip lifts the head 13 to 22 cm off the spine, and South Park's raised arms meet its wide head.

`review_coastal_expression.py` now compares everything outside these features with `4767b7805`.

The per-avatar pillows and duvets were fitted to the old heads. The new hair sinks 1.4 to 3.3 cm into them. A refit would also move each duvet hem 3.6 to 18 cm toward the chin, because the old collar sheets reached further down the back. `bin/refresh_coastal_bedding.py`, followed by the sleep room's bake and export, refits them.

These are original authored meshes, measured on actual Blender poses. The 2D portraits are Sirui's own and were used as visual reference only; no generated imagery was added. Matched before and after renders and comparison sheets are in `.jekyll-cache/visual-qa/character-likeness/`.
