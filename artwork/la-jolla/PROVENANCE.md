## October 26 PC pass: underwater relief and building footprints

The water now uses an actual submerged mesh and a radiance/depth target. Its shelf, canyon and reef shelves interpret the compressed scene's shoreline; they are not a surveyed bathymetric model. The [Scripps Earthguide bathymetric map](https://earthguide.ucsd.edu/eoc/teachers/t_basics/t_maps/p_lajollacanyon.html) identifies the La Jolla–Scripps canyon system used for that geographic relationship.

Light transport follows the distinction between absorption, out-scattering and in-scattering in Pharr, Jakob and Humphreys, [_Physically Based Rendering_, fourth edition, Transmittance](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Volume_Scattering/Transmittance) and [Phase Functions](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Volume_Scattering/Phase_Functions). The implementation integrates a homogeneous column with one directional source and constant ambient illumination; it is not a multiple-scattering or photon-traced solution. RGB coefficients are authored in scene units. Surface reflection uses Schlick's water Fresnel response without the earlier view-normalized strength boost; surface normals follow the geometric wave derivatives plus smaller wind ripples. Mark Finch's [_GPU Gems_, Effective Water Simulation from Physical Models](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models) was consulted for the relationship between geometric waves and finer normal detail. No reference implementation was copied.

The PC study actually viewed Phillip Colla's [offshore Mount Soledad panorama, #27086](https://www.oceanlight.com/info.php?img=27086), and [Torrey Pines sandstone cliffs, #14725](https://www.oceanlight.com/spotlight.php?img=14725), as local reference images. The panorama shows dense pale building fronts against darker inhabited slopes; the cliff photograph shows layered sandstone, vertical erosion ribs and a wet-sand reflection. California State Parks and the California Geological Survey's [_Geological Gems of California State Parks_, GeoGem Note 51](https://www.parks.ca.gov/pages/734/files/GeoGems%20FINAL%20WEB.pdf) describes the layered Torrey Sandstone/Del Mar formations and marine terraces. These observations guide material and relief choices; no photo pixels are textures or committed review assets. Local reference copies remain ignored.

The USGS raster was retrieved again on this PC, and its visible-relief image now samples 0.25° bearings by 0.04° elevation (929×147). Sixteen bounded requests to OpenStreetMap's primary map API supplied actual building polygons after the Overpass services returned errors or timed out. The source area contains 15,075 building footprints; 2,076 pass the bay-visibility check, and 1,200 are selected. Their facade widths/depths come from polygon projection. Eleven selected records have height or levels tags; the other 1,189 use an explicit 6 m display assumption. The glyphs interpret those dimensions and roof tags, with a pixel-size clamp; they are not measured replicas or live occupancy information.

`skyline/provenance.json` now records the USGS request/hash, all sixteen OSM tile URLs, retrieval times and raw hashes, the normalized polygon data hash, selection counts and height assumptions. This is a sequence of tile reads, not an atomic snapshot. Raw TIFF, compressed XML tiles and normalized polygons stay under `.jekyll-cache/coastal-skyline/`. The derived skyline and facade dimensions remain © OpenStreetMap contributors under [ODbL 1.0](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright); USGS elevation remains public domain. The October 24 counts above document the earlier version retained in Git.
