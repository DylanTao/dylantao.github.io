# Two bathrooms in the coastal home

Sirui asked for a proper bathroom on the living floor and a private one beside the bedroom, with enough space and design attention to feel part of the house. The addition carves a stacked wing into the existing mainland rather than placing a detached room beside it. The main entrance, inter-storey stair, bed contacts and onsen remain connected to their original circulation.

The guest bathroom uses honed warm stone and porcelain. Upstairs, celadon ceramic and a stone basin give the en suite a quieter green accent. Both have floating fluted-oak vanities, hollow curved basins, recessed bronze-edged mirrors, restrained linen storage and soap, screened wall-hung WCs, and curbless showers with niches and linear drains. The glass has a fine exposed bronze stile: its collision surface also needs a visible edge. One front wardrobe bay is shortened to make the bedroom's dressing threshold usable.

The delivered geometry adds 78,364 gzip bytes for both bathroom files and 6,023 gzip bytes for the modified shell and wardrobe export. Four bounded line lights supply the mirror washes and concealed shower coves. The existing eight native practical lights and ten analytic point lights remain within their original capacities; the new lighting adds no shadow maps or rendering passes. Each floor has its own indirect-light probes and bounded direct-light region. Four transport-only midpoint sources carry cove energy into cached diffuse bounce.

Independent tests decode the actual Draco exports and use the runtime capsule solver. Fourteen checks pass: both room approaches and return journeys, WC and shower access, actual shower-glass blocking, corridor privacy, hidden-slab and cliff-roof support, and the existing entrance/rooftop routes. The framed openings measure 1.108 m downstairs and 1.155 m upstairs, with minimum measured route headroom of 2.278 m. These are virtual-scene measurements, not building-code certification.

Those checks caught thin wardrobe panels, isolated plant leaves and stems, a stool obstructing circulation, a tight WC turn and transparent glass that was initially classified as walk-through. The actual meshes, placement and glass tags were corrected. A native render also caught a material-name mismatch: “sea-glass ceramic” accidentally inherited glass treatment. The opaque accent is now named celadon ceramic. The exact final baked exports pass the same tests.

Browser review caught two rendering defects that the native area-light previews could not show. Draco placed the floor about 20 micrometres below a strict light-region boundary, and nearest-neighbour distance moments created abrupt angular patches across flat walls and tiles. A 5 cm bounded receiver tolerance and seam-safe bilinear moment sampling correct those defects. Decoded colours were uniformly white and broad-face normals were correct; neither needed altering. Exact zero-weight probe corners now skip their texture reads.

Mirrors reflect their actual rooms through a shared linear render target: 128 pixels on compact touch screens, 256 otherwise. Only the active bathroom captures; ordinary camera movement is capped at 15 Hz and a still view does not recapture. A room or lighting change refreshes immediately. The outdoor sky contributes no mirror reflection. This adds one bounded pass while inspecting a bathroom, with existing shadow maps reused.

The upper floor also forms the lower bathroom ceiling. Cutaway views retain its physical support; first-person walking keeps the space enclosed. A roof tag takes precedence over a room-specific cutaway tag so whole-home views can reveal the upper room correctly. The sketch generator and room-count checks now read the manifest, and the first-view budget includes every base room and default furniture model, rather than only the largest room.
