## What is there

- **A protected reserve.** The water off La Jolla Cove is the Matlahuayl State Marine Reserve, named for Kumeyaay heritage. It was formerly the San Diego-La Jolla Underwater Park Ecological Reserve, and is part of a group of marine protected areas along La Jolla (California State Parks). Its bottom mixes kelp forest, sand flats, rocky reef, and the head of the La Jolla Submarine Canyon, which drops steeply close to shore.
- **Giant kelp.** A sheltered kelp forest: tall stalks from rocky bottom to a canopy floating at the surface. Warm water hurts it; long spells above about 70 °F (21 °C) make it die back.
- **Garibaldi.** The bright orange California state marine fish, a resident of the kelp and reef. Its orange is the site's own colour.
- **Leopard sharks.** They gather in the shallows over sand, the best-known sight of La Jolla's water; sources tie them to the warm months.
- **Others named across sources:** sea lions (the Cove is famous for them, and the scene already has them above water), octopus, California spiny lobster, kelp bass, blue-banded gobies, nudibranchs, giant sea bass (in the neighbouring South La Jolla reserve), dolphins and migrating whales offshore. Grunion spawn on the beaches.

## What it looks like

- **Not tropical.** The water is cold and full of nutrients and plankton, so it is green-blue rather than clear turquoise, and visibility is short. Typical figures: 10 to 20 ft (3 to 6 m) on average, 30 to 40 ft (9 to 12 m) on good days.
- **Seasons.** Clearest in late summer and autumn, once plankton blooms ease and before winter rains. Water roughly 58 to 72 °F (14 to 22 °C), warmest in late summer; the surface can be ten degrees warmer than 60 ft down.
- **Light.** Sunlight comes down through the kelp canopy in shafts; the floor is sand with rock and the kelp's holdfasts.

## What it means for the build

- Underwater fog should close in at about 6 to 12 m, green-blue, denser in spring, clearest in autumn.
- Kelp is the forest: tall stipes from rocky bottom to a canopy that floats and sways with the swell; light shafts through gaps in the canopy.
- Garibaldi are the accent, a few bright orange fish near the rock, echoing the site's colour; schools of smaller, silvery fish turn together in the open.
- Leopard sharks over the sand in the shallows, in the warm months only; sea lions pass through, as they do at the Cove.
- The canyon's edge, if the scene reaches it, is the one dark, deep place: the sense of depth beyond the reef.
