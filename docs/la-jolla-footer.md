# La Jolla, beside Connect and across the footer

Sirui's September 14 plan places a compressed coastal miniature on a translucent atlas beside Connect, with a related landscape filling the bottom of human reading pages. The larger viewer and process live at `/projects/la-jolla/`; the Projects index includes its own card. On mobile the Connect miniature follows the text. AI profiles, redirects, and the secret globe remain undecorated.

## Composition and provenance

The composition brings together DIB's folded glass bays, Geisel Library, Salk's courtyard, Scripps Pier, the Cove, cliff villas, Spanish houses, palms, tennis, volleyball, surfers, and beach life. It deliberately compresses landmark distances. The map below uses actual OpenStreetMap coastline and roads, with a translucent feathered edge and OpenStreetMap/ODbL attribution. It is not a geographically accurate model of building locations.

Coordinated generated front/back studies were passed through a local Hunyuan3D-2mv shape reconstruction. The raw mesh and its actual Blender clay render are retained. Its softened architectural details made it a shape study; the production architecture is deliberately authored in Blender. Source images retain actual `gpt-image` version `2.0` C2PA metadata. Sources, licenses, editable `.blend` files, downloads, and reconstruction details are recorded in [provenance](../artwork/la-jolla/PROVENANCE.md).

## Light, activity, and reveal

Connect centers Geisel and Salk, with a smaller DIB campus, Torrey pines and a rocky Cove. The footer has three unequal groups joined by continuous terrain: Salk on a forward bluff and Geisel farther inland; the existing home/studio neighborhood and courts; then Brockton Villa beside a recessed Cove, La Valencia above the park, and Children's Pool at the outer headland. Changes in setback, orientation and height break the rejected evenly spaced lineup. The compact atlas retains its separate composition. Mainland and beach extend to ±82 units, beyond the camera edges. The tennis court has a retaining foundation; Geisel has a buried podium, central core and paired concrete buttresses. DIB has five folded bays, with the office in bay three at the third floor, plus landward cladding, a gallery and an external stair informed by Sirui's additional photographs. The atlas has an irregular blurred SVG mask and an element mask that fades before the rotated image reaches its crop boundary.

Both scenes follow the selected site theme. The footer moves from quiet morning to midday surfing and courts, warm afternoon activity, then an evening bonfire with parked boards and lit windows. The campus miniature keeps its quieter Cove and courtyard composition, with surfing during the day and illuminated windows at night. The DIB third-floor office lights from noon onward and on a stable subset of nights. This is a personal vignette, not live occupancy information.

Terrain is always present. The first live frame keeps the preview's complete composition; downloading the model must not shrink the coast or restart a building entrance. After an upward scroll, building groups lower and emerge left to right according to footer visibility on subsequent passes. There is no scroll interception. The coast fills the viewport width and reaches the bottom edge; copyright sits quietly inside it. Sirui removed the al-folio credit, update date, and separate lighting/motion controls.

On wide displays, the camera expands sideways while retaining at least 14.5 world units of vertical clearance. A level horizon keeps the inland roofs and seaward breakwater inside the frame. The outer shoreline carries architecture researched from map outlines, satellite views, official building plans and photographs. The physical relationships matter as much as the individual models: curving paths connect entries and overlooks, the hotel sits behind the park, and a stair anchors the breakwater to land. Porch tables, warm windows and flowering pots retain the domestic scale. The Connect miniature keeps its own camera and geometry. Homepage content grows to 2240 px on a 4K display, while the section rail stays near the left edge and moves or hides before reaching the footer.

Reveal, camera settling, and authored motion use active elapsed time instead of a per-frame time cap. Exponential easing keeps the response consistent when frames are slow. Offscreen and hidden-page recovery reset the clock, so suspension does not advance the vignette. Browser reversal checks place the page at the reveal boundary before measuring the response; they do not include the separate page-level smooth-scroll duration.

The customized footer uses `footer_fixed: false`. The previous fixed-footer branch left a masked band and a separate bottom gap even when the scene itself measured full width. Acceptance now checks the actual bottom edge and absence of that mask, as well as width.

The Connect and project miniatures allow gentle bounded orbit with dragging or arrow keys; Home resets the view. On narrow screens, the footer permits horizontal exploration across both landmark wings while vertical touch gestures retain page scrolling. The camera rises gradually when visiting the inland landmarks. Left/right arrow keys pan, and Home returns to the studio and beach. Reduced motion keeps a composed still view and deliberate camera interaction. Offscreen and hidden-document render loops stop.

## People on the coast

Sirui's October 18 review called the figures "really weird and static". Everyone in the footer now uses the prop they belong to, keeps the miniature's art style (rods, a tapered torso, ellipsoid heads and hands, no faces) and moves in gentle loops driven by the scene clock:

- Two players hold a crosscourt tennis rally. The ball clears the net, bounces in the receiver's half and meets a racket at every contact. The receiver shuffles to the ball, turns their shoulders and swings a forehand or a backhand. Two volleyball players bump a high pass over the net.
- A dog walker strolls the wet sand from the courts to the pier. They pause at each end to look out to sea, then turn back, while the dog trots ahead, circles round and sniffs. A walker uses the campus bluff walk at midday, and a jogger in the morning. In the evening, a couple walks the waterline holding hands, one half a step ahead so both read from the sea.
- Visitors sit on the four overlook benches and on a DIB terrace bench: one reads, one leans back on both hands, one rests forearms on their knees, one types on a laptop, and one watches the sea. Heads turn in slow, held glances.
- At night three people sit on driftwood logs around the fire. Two warm their hands and rub them now and then; one roasts a marshmallow. The flames flicker from their base, and a short-range warm light falls on the sitters and the sand.
- One surfer rides crouched with arms out, one sits astride a board facing out to sea, and one paddles a slow loop. A swimmer swims freestyle along the shore, and a sunbather reads on a towel under a parasol, feet swinging.

People keep the composed pose the posters show (scene time zero) under reduced motion, the site's Pause motion, and automation, as the cinematic layer does. `?cinematic=live` lets a browser test watch them move. Offscreen and hidden pages stop their clock with the rest of the coast. Figures do not cast shadows into the on-demand shadow map, where a moving person would leave a stale shadow behind. Each figure carries a soft contact shadow instead, offset in the sun's direction. Players appear only once their court has fully risen during the scroll reveal.

The same review fixed geometry at its source: the fire ring sat in the surf and on the volleyball court beside a parasol, the volleyball court reached past the waterline, and the DIB stair ran into the sea. The pier stopped in mid-air above the sand, the lifeguard hut was half buried in the bluff, and the villa stair ran into the hill. The Library approach looped down the Cove's face, and the promenade passed under Brockton Villa's stone terrace. The Children's Pool stair tunnelled through the cliff, the hillside came up through a tennis court corner, and a palm's fronds cut through a cottage ridge. Sage bushes grew through the villa terrace, benches stood at twice seat height with visitors standing on them, and a surfer drifted through the pier.

## The sea

Sirui's October 18 request asked for better water "at 3d screen and bottom of page", with two references: a stylized teal harbour whose near-mirror water carries soft ripples, long lamp streaks and mist, and a clear Mediterranean cove where the sandy, rocky seabed shows through turquoise shallows under moving caustics. The footer's sea (`water.mjs`) now does the following at footer scale:

- **Clear shallows over a real-looking seabed.** There is no geometry under the water, so the shader follows each pixel's view ray through the rippled surface (Snell's law) down to an analytic seabed: a sandy shelf that deepens quickly off the beach and then slowly, the head of the La Jolla Canyon dropping close to shore off Scripps Pier (as Scripps Canyon does there), and dark reef rock off the coves and headlands beside the wave-worn bluff feet. Beer–Lambert absorption takes red first, so the sand turns aqua, then turquoise, then the open water's own color. Animated caustics, the cell borders of a moving Worley lattice, brighten the shallow bed by day and rest at night.
- **Surf that follows the beach.** Crests travel shoreward once every nine seconds, close up as they shoal and break into lacy whitewater over the shelf; reefs break them farther out, and a shore-break line brightens as each wave arrives. The riding and paddling boards and the swimmer trail Kelvin wakes, whose arms open at 19.47 degrees.
- **A mirrored coast, drawn on demand.** A small render of the scene from the camera's mirror image below the water (`aimMirror`) covers only the band of the view where water shows. Like the shadow map, it is redrawn only when the theme, size, reveal or camera changes, so a steady frame costs no extra draw calls. Ripples sway it sideways and stretch it along the view, and only light brighter than a theme threshold streaks far, so walls reflect softly while lit windows, the DIB office and lamps draw long streaks. People, boards and the sea itself stay out of the mirror, where a moving figure would leave a stale reflection.
- **Evening lamps and glitter.** Three slim lamp posts stand on Scripps Pier with their heads on arms over the water. By day they are dark; at night their bulbs glow, and they and the fire each lay a glitter path from their mirror point toward the viewer, lit only on ripple facets tilted back toward the light and flickering with the fire.
- **Haze toward the skyline.** The farthest hills soften into the page color behind the canvas; the sea and the coast stay crisp.
- **A legible credit.** Reflections, glitter and surf pass behind the copyright line, so it carries a halo that follows the hour (see the sky below). The ring of pixels around its glyphs measures at least 4.5:1 at the median in every theme and at every sunset and dusk minute checked.

Reduced motion, Pause motion and offscreen pages hold the composed still (scene time zero), as before; automation still sees the sea move while people hold their pose.

## The sky, the sun and one clock

Sirui's second October 18 review asked for a well-designed halo that changes through the day, a better sunset and reflection ("sunset near pacific ocean is gorgeous and you can have data on when is sunset in lajolla easily"), the haze kept, fog and light to play with, the dusk window solved from first principles, and the Connect page brought along.

- **One sun clock.** `assets/js/la-jolla-sun.mjs` computes La Jolla's sun (32.83 N, 117.27 W) with NOAA's general solar position equations: altitude, azimuth, sunrise and sunset at -0.833 degrees, civil twilight and solar noon, for the visitor's date, with the visitor's clock read as La Jolla's. It matches sunrise-sunset.org within 20 seconds (2026-09-28) and the 3D home's `daylight.mjs` to floating-point precision. The site theme now turns on this sun rather than at 5, 11, 15 and 20 o'clock: noon is the four hours around solar noon, and evening begins when the sun is 3 degrees down, the altitude at which light ink reads as well as dark on the coast behind the credit (measured on the afterglow, 2026-10-19). `theme.js` carries an exact copy for the head, before any module loads; `test/coast-sky.test.mjs` holds the two equal for every day of 2026 and 2027. The sun arc maps La Jolla's day onto its drawn arc (`sun-arc-math.mjs`, `toReference`), so dusk meets the horizon at the minute the site turns evening and solar noon stands at the crown; the four stops stand inside their modes on every date. A chosen theme still shows its authored look, so the stills stay true.
- **Looks by solar altitude.** `footer-coast/sky-look.mjs` blends authored looks by the sun's altitude: golden hour (6 degrees), sunset (0.5), afterglow (-3), the blue hour (-8) and night going down; predawn, dawn and morning coming up. The four theme looks remain exact anchors, renders of them are unchanged against main, and the coast returns to them at high noon and in deep night. The footer looks south-west across the bay, so the setting sun is behind the coast: the key light swings behind it, raking the bluffs and throwing long shadows toward the viewer, and the studio light that lit every surface evenly dims with the hour (three r164 applies a scene environment at `scene.environmentIntensity`, not each material's `envMapIntensity`, so the old per-material 0.22 never took effect). At dawn the rising sun lights the coast from the front.
- **A Pacific sunset.** `footer-coast/sky.mjs` paints, in one screen-space layer at the far plane, the sky and the open sea beyond the coast. It composites in display colors and inverts the output pass's ACES curve, so the authored gold, apricot, rose and violet land as authored. The sun stands over the sea horizon by its real altitude and across the view by its real azimuth (in October right of the DIB, in December nearer the middle, in June far right), flattens by refraction near the horizon, reddens through the marine layer's haze bank and sinks into the sea, laying a glitter path of short dashes across it. Its glow and rays outlast it into the afterglow. Looking west at dawn, the Earth's blue-grey shadow lies on the sea under the pink Belt of Venus.
- **Reflections.** The near sea carries the sun's glitter path, a sparkle column on ripple facets over a soft sheen, from the shore toward the viewer. The mirror now leaves its sky clear, and the sea fills it with the hour's sky: the low, warm sky near the beach and the higher sky in the foreground, as a perspective view would show.
- **The marine layer and its light.** `footer-coast/marine-layer.mjs` lays low fog over the surf line and the beach in three stacked sheets, depth-tested so the coast stands in it. Each sheet reads the sun's shadow map, so the fog stays dim where palms and buildings block the low sun and glows where light passes: at golden hour, shafts reach across the beach toward the viewer. It scatters forward (Henyey-Greenstein), brightest toward the sun, and is densest at dawn and dusk; the theme anchors carry none.
- **Lights coming on.** Windows, the DIB office, the pier lamps and their glitter brighten through the afterglow on the same altitude scale, before the evening fire.
- **A halo for the hour.** The credit's halo (`creditHalo`, CSS custom properties `--coast-halo` and `--coast-glow`) is a faint veil at noon, a warm cream veil with a golden glow at sunset, rose in the afterglow, and at night a navy veil under light ink with a cool moonlit glow. It eases between hours and holds still under reduced motion and Pause motion. The credit stays: it is the site's only signature, and the halo keeps it at least 4.5:1 at the median over the moving sea.
- **The Connect miniature.** It now runs the same sea on its relief's water slab (`water.mjs` `SEAS`), with its own cove shoreline, a canyon off its pier and reefs off the headland rocks, so it has the clear shallows, surf, wakes, mirrored landmarks and caustics too. `build_la_jolla_miniature.py` was rerun in Blender: its riders are no longer baked, the browser poses them on their boards as on the footer, and the static surf lines gave way to the shader's surf. Its four stills were rendered again.

Reduced motion, Pause motion and offscreen pages keep the sky, the fog and the sea still with the rest of the coast.

## Implementation and budgets

- `_includes/la-jolla-miniature.liquid`, `_includes/la-jolla-footer.liquid`, `_sass/_footer-coast.scss`, and `_includes/footer.liquid` own integration and layout.
- `bin/coastal_life.py` is the single placement source for beach life: courts, the fire circle and logs, benches, parasols, towels, the lifeguard tower, the DIB beach stair and the pier landing, plus every person's seat, path, court or board. It needs no Blender, so `test/test_coastal_life.py` checks props against the terrain, the surf and each other, and confirms that the committed manifest matches it. `build_la_jolla.py` writes its figures into `manifest.json`.
- `assets/js/footer-coast/people-pose.mjs` holds pure posture math: a small two-bone reach for arms and legs, gaits, seated actions, the rally and the dog. `people.mjs` draws every person, prop and contact shadow as five instanced meshes (rods, torsos, ellipsoids, boxes, shadows), so the cast costs a fixed handful of draw calls whatever its size. People outside the camera's horizontal span are not posed, and sitters re-pose every third frame. While animating, the cast costs about 0.4 ms of main-thread time per frame (October 18 CPU profile, 1440-wide footer). A still pose costs only re-placing the riders on their moving boards. `test/coast-people.test.mjs` samples the poses over time.
- `assets/js/footer-coast/entry.mjs` imports the renderer near visibility (450 px), using its content-versioned URL from the page. `assets.mjs` shares decoder, model resources, and geometry; instances have their own materials and light/activity state.
- `scene.mjs` uses the existing pinned Three.js stack, studio reflections, soft sun shadows, bounded contact occlusion, and authored water/plant/character motion. It caps device pixel density at 1.5 and animation at approximately 30 fps.
- `assets/js/footer-coast/water.mjs` draws the sea as one plane and one shader, built from the pure math in `water-optics.mjs` (seabed depth, absorption, refraction, Fresnel, surf phase and breaking, wake angle, mirror points, the on-demand schedule), which `test/coast-water.test.mjs` checks. The waterline reaches the shader as a 1,024-texel strip sampled from the exported profile. Loop bounds are uniforms and hashes are short (lowbias32 over Teschner's spatial-hash primes) because ANGLE's Direct3D compiler unrolls constant-bound loops and inlines every call: the first version compiled in 1.9 s cold against 0.6 s for the old water, the shipped one in about 1.1 times the old. The mirror is a half-float target at about half the canvas density over the water band only; it redraws at most every other frame while the view or the buildings change and not at all in a steady frame. Measured October 18 against main ec8cdb5ac on a bare harness (headless Chromium, ANGLE/D3D11, this laptop's Intel Arc iGPU, medians of five interleaved pairs): at 1440 × 345 the frame stays at the 30 fps cap with 294 draws instead of 291 (the lamp meshes), 2.33 ms of main-thread work per frame against 2.40 and 6.8 ms of GPU time against 6.5; at 390 × 330 (density capped at 1.5) 198 draws instead of 195, 1.86 ms against 1.81 and 6.0 ms of GPU against 5.3, and at night 2.13 ms against 1.95 with 6.3 ms of GPU against 5.6. The first frame draws the mirror once (1,352 draws at 1440 against 1,202), and cold first frames stay level within this shared machine's noise.
- The pier lamps are two instanced meshes (posts with arms, bulbs); the haze is three's linear fog from just past the coast to the hills behind, aimed at a lifted page color because the output pass tone-maps after fog, and at the sky's haze (through the same inverse tone curve) while the sky shows.
- The sky is one full-screen triangle pair at the far plane, drawn only while the hour shows a sky; the marine layer is three transparent sheets that discard outside their band; both stay out of the mirror and the contact pass. The footer and the miniature share one sea program: each sea's waterline mapping, canyon and reefs are uniforms. Both scenes compile their programs on the driver's worker threads before the first frame (`renderer.compile` under the finish's half-float target, then readiness polls through `scheduler.postTask`, which a paused test clock does not freeze, bounded at about eight seconds), and nothing draws meanwhile. Measured October 19 against main dcff1a5db on the bare harness (headless Chromium, ANGLE/D3D11, this laptop's Intel Arc iGPU, interleaved pairs, medians; GPU timer queries were unavailable on this run): every steady frame stays at the 30 fps cap; noon draws are unchanged (294 at 1440, 198 at 390) and a sunset adds four (the sky and three fog sheets); main-thread time per frame stays within this shared machine's noise (1440 noon 4.5 against 4.6 ms; 1440 sunset 5.7 against 6.2 ms over five pairs). The miniature draws 75 instead of 84. Cold starts in a fresh GPU process got shorter: the footer at sunset reaches its first frame in 2.3 s instead of 2.7 s with a longest main-thread task of 0.74 s instead of 1.33 s, and the miniature, whose new sea alone had added 0.84 s of blocking compile, in 2.2 s instead of 2.8 s with 0.94 s instead of 1.19 s.
- `coastal_terrain.py` authors the ground at its final width; there is no runtime terrain stretch. The exported shoreline profile drives the water shader as well as the beaches and bluffs. Architecture, paths, veranda, seawall and seals are authored and batched in Blender; the miniature's separate composition is retained.
- `bin/build_la_jolla.py`, `coastal_landmarks.py`, `coastal_campus.py`, `coastal_panorama.py`, `coastal_terrain.py`, and `build_la_jolla_miniature.py` retain editable sources. Repeated authoring primitives reuse finished shapes with independent mesh data before batching, avoiding repeated Blender scene updates. The two compressed GLBs total about 1.8 MB, within the approximate 4 MB combined geometry/atlas/poster target. Concept boards and the raw reconstruction are downloads, not initial scene resources.
- `bin/render_coast_posters.cjs` captures the actual runtime camera, extended panorama, materials, and all four themes. The coast has a wide still and a narrow DIB-centered still; both miniature sizes share a fixed-width still. The responsive crop follows the live camera, including the tablet's minimum world width. The wide still includes extra sky and water for those taller frames. Twelve WebP files total about 695 KB (the coast's stills at quality 80 since the clear sea, whose ripples cost bytes; the miniature's at 88); a visitor loads only the applicable view and theme. Regenerate all variants together: the wide noon still supplies their shared cache version. The original Blender renders remain authoring/process assets.
- The composed still remains visible without JavaScript, WebGL, or successful model decoding. The entry module selects its theme without importing the renderer. Once a complete frame is drawn, it appears underneath the still, which fades away over 240 ms. Reduced motion switches directly. Graphics failure restores the still immediately; context recovery draws before announcing readiness. There are no controls for unavailable graphics.

## Verification

`test/visual/footer-coast.spec.js` covers four themes, full viewport width, nonblank and changing pixels, lazy loading, automatic activity, reversible reveal, offscreen recovery, keyboard and touch orbit, native vertical scrolling, reduced motion, context loss, and failed-model/no-JavaScript fallbacks. Loading checks hold the real model request, compare the still to the first complete frame at the four standard viewports plus 4K, and sample the animated handoff to reject a blank canvas or collapsing buildings. The 4K check also projects the actual landmark bounds through the camera and rejects clipped roofs or seawalls. Each theme check measures the credit's contrast in a 2 px ring around its glyphs (median at least 4.5:1), and the sea check confirms that the mirror is drawn once, not again while the coast animates, and once more for a new theme, with the fire and pier lamps glittering at night. The sky check follows the clock through La Jolla's sunset on 2026-10-19: eight minutes before it the afternoon theme holds while the sun's disk stands over the sea and the marine layer lies along the surf, and at 18:30 the theme, the sun arc's label and the credit's ink all read evening, with the credit at least 4.5:1 at both. `test/coast-sky.test.mjs` checks the sun against almanac times, the head's copy of the theme clock against the module for two years of dates, the arc's mapping, the anchors, the continuity of the light through three days, and the inverse tone curve. Use one owned server and one worker during review.
