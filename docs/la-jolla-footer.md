# La Jolla, beside Connect and across the footer

Sirui's September 14 plan places a compressed coastal miniature on a translucent atlas beside Connect, with a related landscape filling the bottom of human reading pages. The larger viewer and process live at `/projects/la-jolla/`; the Projects index includes its own card. On mobile the Connect miniature follows the text. AI profiles, redirects, and the secret globe remain undecorated.

## Composition and provenance

The composition brings together DIB's folded glass bays, Geisel Library, Salk's courtyard, Scripps Pier, the Cove, cliff villas, Spanish houses, palms, tennis, volleyball, surfers, and beach life. It deliberately compresses landmark distances. The map below uses actual OpenStreetMap coastline and roads, with a translucent feathered edge and OpenStreetMap/ODbL attribution. It is not a geographically accurate model of building locations.

Coordinated generated front/back studies were passed through a local Hunyuan3D-2mv shape reconstruction. The raw mesh and its actual Blender clay render are retained. Its softened architectural details made it a shape study; the production architecture is deliberately authored in Blender. Source images retain actual `gpt-image` version `2.0` C2PA metadata. Sources, licenses, editable `.blend` files, downloads, and reconstruction details are recorded in [provenance](../artwork/la-jolla/PROVENANCE.md).

## Light, activity, and reveal

Connect centers Geisel and Salk, with a smaller DIB campus, Torrey pines and a rocky Cove. The footer has three unequal groups joined by continuous terrain: Salk on a forward bluff and Geisel farther inland; the existing home/studio neighborhood and courts; then Brockton Villa beside a recessed Cove, La Valencia above the park, and Children's Pool at the outer headland. Changes in setback, orientation and height break the rejected evenly spaced lineup. The compact atlas retains its separate composition. Mainland and beach extend to ±82 units, beyond the camera edges. The tennis court has a retaining foundation; Geisel has a buried podium, central core and paired concrete buttresses. DIB has five folded bays, with the office in bay three at the third floor, plus landward cladding, a gallery and an external stair informed by Sirui's additional photographs. The atlas has an irregular blurred SVG mask and an element mask that fades before the rotated image reaches its crop boundary.

Both scenes follow the selected site theme. The footer moves from quiet morning to midday surfing and courts, warm afternoon activity, then an evening bonfire with parked boards and lit windows. The campus miniature keeps its quieter Cove and courtyard composition, with surfing during the day and illuminated windows at night. The DIB third-floor office lights from noon onward and on a stable subset of nights. This is a personal vignette, not live occupancy information.

Terrain is always present. The first live frame keeps the preview's complete composition; downloading the model must not shrink the coast or restart a building entrance. After an upward scroll, building groups lower and emerge left to right according to footer visibility on subsequent passes. There is no scroll interception. The coast fills the viewport width and reaches the bottom edge; copyright sits quietly inside it. Sirui removed the al-folio credit, update date, and separate lighting/motion controls.

On wide displays, the camera expands sideways while retaining at least 14.5 world units of vertical clearance. A level horizon keeps the inland roofs and seaward breakwater inside the frame. The outer shoreline carries architecture researched from map outlines, satellite views, official building plans and photographs. The physical relationships matter as much as the individual models: curving paths connect entries and overlooks, the hotel sits behind the park, and a stair anchors the breakwater to land. Porch tables, warm windows and flowering pots retain the domestic scale. The Connect miniature keeps its own camera and geometry. Homepage content grows to 2240 px on a 4K display, while the section rail stays near the left edge and moves or hides before reaching the footer.

Reveal, camera settling, and authored motion use active elapsed time instead of a per-frame time cap. Exponential easing keeps the response consistent when frames are slow. Offscreen and hidden-page recovery reset the clock, so suspension does not advance the vignette. Browser reversal checks place the page at the reveal boundary before measuring the response; they do not include the separate page-level smooth-scroll duration.

The customized footer uses `footer_fixed: false`. The previous fixed-footer branch left a masked band and a separate bottom gap even when the scene itself measured full width. Acceptance now checks the actual bottom edge and absence of that mask, as well as width.

The Connect and project miniatures allow gentle bounded orbit with dragging or arrow keys; Home resets the view. On narrow screens, the footer permits horizontal exploration across both landmark wings while vertical touch gestures retain page scrolling. The camera rises gradually when visiting the inland landmarks. Left/right arrow keys pan, and Home returns to the studio and beach. Reduced motion keeps a composed still view and deliberate camera interaction. Offscreen and hidden-document render loops stop.

## People on the coast

Sirui's October 18 review called the figures "really weird and static". Everyone in the footer now uses the prop they belong to, keeps the miniature's art style (rods, a tapered torso, ellipsoid heads and hands, no faces) and moves in gentle loops driven by the scene clock:

- Two players hold a crosscourt tennis rally. The ball clears the net, bounces in the receiver's half and meets a racket at every contact. The receiver shuffles to the ball, turns their shoulders and swings a forehand or a backhand. Two volleyball players bump a high pass over the net.
- A dog walker strolls the wet sand from the courts to the pier. They pause at each end to look out to sea, then turn back, while the dog trots ahead, circles round and sniffs. A walker uses the campus bluff walk at midday, and a jogger in the morning. In the evening, a couple walks the waterline holding hands, one half a step ahead so both read from the sea.
- Visitors sit on the four overlook benches and on a DIB terrace bench: one reads, one leans back on both hands, one rests forearms on their knees, one types on a laptop, and one watches the sea. Heads turn in slow, held glances.
- At night three people sit on driftwood logs around the fire. Two warm their hands and rub them now and then; one roasts a marshmallow. The flames flicker from their base, and a short-range warm light falls on the sitters and the sand.
- One surfer rides crouched with arms out, one sits astride a board facing out to sea, and one paddles a slow loop. A swimmer swims freestyle along the shore, and a sunbather reads on a towel under a parasol, feet swinging.

People keep the composed pose the posters show (scene time zero) under reduced motion, the site's Pause motion, and automation, as the cinematic layer does. `?cinematic=live` lets a browser test watch them move. Offscreen and hidden pages stop their clock with the rest of the coast. Figures do not cast shadows into the on-demand shadow map, where a moving person would leave a stale shadow behind. Each figure carries a soft contact shadow instead, offset in the sun's direction. Players appear only once their court has fully risen during the scroll reveal.

The same review fixed geometry at its source: the fire ring sat in the surf and on the volleyball court beside a parasol, the volleyball court reached past the waterline, and the DIB stair ran into the sea. The pier stopped in mid-air above the sand, the lifeguard hut was half buried in the bluff, and the villa stair ran into the hill. The Library approach looped down the Cove's face, and the promenade passed under Brockton Villa's stone terrace. The Children's Pool stair tunnelled through the cliff, the hillside came up through a tennis court corner, and a palm's fronds cut through a cottage ridge. Sage bushes grew through the villa terrace, benches stood at twice seat height with visitors standing on them, and a surfer drifted through the pier.

## The sea

Sirui's October 18 request asked for better water "at 3d screen and bottom of page", with two references: a stylized teal harbour whose near-mirror water carries soft ripples, long lamp streaks and mist, and a clear Mediterranean cove where the sandy, rocky seabed shows through turquoise shallows under moving caustics. The footer's sea (`water.mjs`) now does the following at footer scale:

- **Geometry beneath clear shallows.** `bed-relief.mjs` builds a submerged sandy shelf, a nearshore canyon and low reef shelves from the compressed scene's own shoreline. The footer has 49,665 vertices and 98,304 triangles below the water; the miniature has its own smaller grid. This is authored relief informed by the La Jolla–Scripps canyon system, not surveyed bathymetry. A full-canvas radiance/depth target renders the actual mesh under the coast's sun and sky. The water follows each Snell-refracted view ray to the same relief grid and samples that rendered bottom. Beer–Lambert extinction includes absorption and out-scattering. Constant ambient illumination and one directional source are integrated through a homogeneous water column, with a Henyey–Greenstein phase function for the directional contribution. The coefficients are authored in compressed scene units, not measurements of today's water. Animated Worley caustics remain an approximation and rest at night; this is single scattering, without multiple scattering or photon-traced caustics. The optics references and source boundary are in [provenance](../artwork/la-jolla/PROVENANCE.md).
- **Surf that follows the beach.** Crests travel shoreward once every nine seconds, close up as they shoal and break into lacy whitewater over the shelf; reefs break them farther out, and a shore-break line brightens as each wave arrives. The riding and paddling boards and the swimmer trail Kelvin wakes, whose arms open at 19.47 degrees.
- **A mirrored coast, drawn on demand.** A small render of the scene from the camera's mirror image below the water (`aimMirror`) covers only the band of the view where water shows. It and the bottom target refresh when the theme, size, reveal or camera changes; a steady view reuses both. Surface reflection uses water's dielectric Fresnel response: about 2% head-on, rising toward grazing angles. The October 26 PC pass removes the old view-normalized boost, which could clamp the sea to 90% mirror strength. Ripples bend the reflection, with shorter along-view distortion. Bright-light streaks and the authored glitter paths remain approximations. People, boards and the sea itself stay out of the cached mirror, where a moving figure would leave a stale reflection.
- **Evening lamps and glitter.** Three slim lamp posts stand on Scripps Pier with their heads on arms over the water. By day they are dark; at night their bulbs glow, and they and the fire each lay a glitter path from their mirror point toward the viewer, lit only on ripple facets tilted back toward the light and flickering with the fire.
- **Haze toward the hills.** The coast's back fades into the air the hills behind it stand in (the page by day, the low sky over the hills at dusk); the sea and the coast stay crisp.
- **A legible credit.** Reflections, glitter and surf pass behind the copyright line, so it carries a halo that follows the hour (see the sky below). The ring of pixels around its glyphs measures at least 4.5:1 at the median in every theme and at every sunset and dusk minute checked.

Reduced motion, Pause motion and offscreen pages hold the composed still (scene time zero), as before; automation still sees the sea move while people hold their pose.

The footer/miniature canvas preserves its drawing buffer. A Windows WebKit before-control on both the earlier source and the PC pass discarded the first reduced-motion frame when the hidden canvas became visible; only the map or page remained until another draw. Preserving the buffer keeps the first complete still painted without running a background animation loop. The review records that engine's generic renderer string separately from the native NVIDIA measurements; it is not physical-phone evidence.

## The sky, the sun and one clock

Sirui's second October 18 review asked for a well-designed halo that changes through the day, a better sunset and reflection ("sunset near pacific ocean is gorgeous and you can have data on when is sunset in lajolla easily"), the haze kept, fog and light to play with, the dusk window solved from first principles, and the Connect page brought along.

- **One sun clock.** `assets/js/la-jolla-sun.mjs` computes La Jolla's sun (32.83 N, 117.27 W) with NOAA's general solar position equations: altitude, azimuth, sunrise and sunset at -0.833 degrees, civil twilight and solar noon, for the visitor's date, with the visitor's clock read as La Jolla's. It matches sunrise-sunset.org within 20 seconds (2026-09-28) and the 3D home's `daylight.mjs` to floating-point precision. The site theme now turns on this sun rather than at 5, 11, 15 and 20 o'clock: noon is the four hours around solar noon, and evening begins when the sun is 3 degrees down, the altitude at which light ink reads as well as dark on the coast behind the credit (measured on the afterglow, 2026-10-19). `theme.js` carries an exact copy for the head, before any module loads; `test/coast-sky.test.mjs` holds the two equal for every day of 2026 and 2027. The sun arc maps La Jolla's day onto its drawn arc (`sun-arc-math.mjs`, `toReference`), so dusk meets the horizon at the minute the site turns evening and solar noon stands at the crown; the four stops stand inside their modes on every date. A chosen theme still shows its authored look, so the stills stay true.
- **Looks by solar altitude.** `footer-coast/sky-look.mjs` blends authored looks for the coast's own light by the sun's altitude: golden hour (6 degrees), sunset (0.5), afterglow (-3), the blue hour (-8) and night going down; predawn, dawn and morning coming up. The four theme looks remain exact anchors, and the coast returns to them at high noon and in deep night. The studio light that lit every surface evenly dims with the hour (three r164 applies a scene environment at `scene.environmentIntensity`, not each material's `envMapIntensity`, so the old per-material 0.22 never took effect).

Sirui's October 24 review asked for a more natural, photoreal transition ("with some sota graphics paper like from siggraph papers") in the sky's gradient, the page-to-sky edge, the change through the hour, and the sun, sea and haze. He also corrected the geography: the band behind the coast looked like a second sea, but the sea is in front, so it should be the hills next to the coast, with fancy houses lit up, "a bit like a silhouette".

- **The frieze faces the land.** The footer is a compressed panorama from a boat in La Jolla Bay, looking at the shore. Torrey Pines mesa and the campus lie to the north-east (left), the hills above La Jolla Shores to the east, Mount Soledad to the south-east behind Scripps Pier, and the Village and the Cove to the south (right). Past Point La Jolla the land ends at the open Pacific, beyond the panorama's reach. `footer-coast/skyline.mjs` maps the frieze's x to compass bearings through the frieze's own landmarks (Salk, Geisel and Brockton Villa keep their real bearings and order); the distances between them are compressed, as the coast's are. The setting sun therefore stands behind the viewer (bearings 242 to 299 through the year) and the rising sun comes up over the hills ahead (61 to 118). The earlier design faced south-west so the sun could sink into the sea; that view could not hold Salk and Geisel on its left. Sirui chose the honest geometry on October 24: no setting sun's disk shows. Instead golden light rakes the coast and Mount Soledad (alpenglow), the pink Belt of Venus rises over the Earth's shadow, the blue hour follows and the hillside houses light up. The far right, toward the hidden sun, glows warmer. At dawn the disk rises over the hills in view and lays its glitter path across the bay.
- **The key light where the sun is.** Low suns light the coast from their real bearing against the frieze's view (104 degrees): the October sunset from behind the viewer and to the right, so seaward faces and the hills glow while long shadows fall inland; the sunrise from behind the hills, rimming the bluffs and throwing shadows toward the viewer. By day and night the authored directions return.
- **The hills, measured.** `bin/coastal_skyline.py` stands a viewer in the bay (32.862 N, 117.266 W, eye 15 m) over USGS 3DEP elevation (The National Map's 3DEPElevation service, about 12 by 14 m cells, public domain). Along each half-degree of bearing it finds the skyline and the nearer hills within 1.7 km. The October 26 PC pass samples the visible relief at 0.25 degrees of bearing and 0.04 degrees of elevation (`assets/models/la-jolla/hills.png`, 929×147, about 61 KB). It reads actual OpenStreetMap building polygons through sixteen bounded map-API requests after Overpass attempts failed. Of 15,075 footprints, 2,076 are visible from the bay and 1,200 are selected. Their projected polygon width and depth, distance, height/levels provenance and roof tags accompany their positions. Only eleven selected buildings have height or levels tags; the others use an explicit 6 m display assumption. These are sequential tile reads, not an atomic historical snapshot. Mount Soledad's ridge rises 5 degrees over the bay and remains behind Scripps Pier. Downloads stay in `.jekyll-cache/coastal-skyline/`; `artwork/la-jolla/skyline/provenance.json` records each exact request and raw-input hash.
- **A physically based sky, after Hillaire 2020.** `footer-coast/atmosphere.mjs` follows Sébastien Hillaire, "A Scalable and Production Ready Sky and Atmosphere Rendering Technique" (EGSR 2020): a transmittance table, a multiple-scattering table (his equation 10, all orders as a geometric series), and a sky-view table of radiance over the directions the frieze shows, in his non-linear latitude. The planet, the media and the transmittance table's parameterization follow Bruneton and Neyret, "Precomputed Atmospheric Scattering" (EGSR 2008), and Bruneton's 2017 reference implementation (BSD 3-Clause). The air is La Jolla's marine air: sea-salt aerosol of optical depth about 0.06 at 550 nm, and ozone's Chappuis band, which keeps the blue hour blue. The equations are implemented here; no code is copied. It was chosen over the alternatives for twilight, cost and license. Each sample tests the planet's shadow toward the sun, and the multiple-scattering table is indexed by the sun's zenith angle at every altitude, so the Earth's shadow and the Belt of Venus emerge from the model (`test/coast-hills.test.mjs` checks both). Bruneton's 2008 model needs four-dimensional tables. The Prague sky model (Wilkie et al., SIGGRAPH 2021) stops at a solar elevation of -4.2 degrees, and its published datasets run from about 100 MB to 2.2 GB. The tables are tiny and built on the CPU when the coast mounts; the sky-view table (48 by 28) is rebuilt only when the sun moves, at most once a minute on the clock. A frame reads it once per pixel.
- **What the layer draws.** `footer-coast/sky.mjs` draws the sky and hills in one pass behind the coast. The plane pans with the coast on narrow viewports and with the pointer's parallax. Terrain shading follows the measured relief normals under sun and sky; the miniature's presentation studio no longer illuminates the distant hills. Aerial perspective still thins with height, with less extinction in the PC pass so the ground separates from the pale houses. Each building is a small facade/roof glyph whose width follows its actual polygon's angular extent and whose height follows an OSM tag or the documented assumption. These remain distant glyphs, not detailed building meshes; projected size is clamped for legibility. Windows occupy the facades rather than making circular glowing points. They light through the same afterglow scale and hold still under reduced motion.
- **The eye's adaptation and the page edge.** The sky's radiance falls about a thousandfold from sunset to civil dusk. The layer exposes it as the eye adapts, 85 % of each stop, so its display brightness falls gently (0.36 at golden hour, 0.28 at sunset, 0.14 at civil dusk). Below about 12.5 degrees the adaptation stops, and the sky darkens into the night page. The colors are tone-mapped as the output pass maps the coast (three r164 ACES), then passed back through the inverse of that curve. By day the page remains the sky. The physical sky gathers from 7 degrees above the horizon to full at sunset, holds through the blue hour, and fades into the night page by nautical dusk. Over the upper half of the band between the horizon and the canvas top it dissolves into the page color in OKLab (Björn Ottosson, 2020), with a smootherstep that leaves no edge at either end, so no line marks where the page begins.
- **Continuous page and dusk.** `theme.js` blends a mode change over 700 ms. At the page head, native view transitions crossfade the complete painted view for the automatic clock and sun arc; manual stop selection keeps its bounded water reveal. At a reading position, or without the API, CSS blends the page colors while a temporary copy of each visible coastal framebuffer carries the coast. The same reading-position path serves every engine: a retained control exposed missing intermediate pixels in scrolled WebKit root snapshots. The copies cannot take input and are removed after completion or cancellation. Reduced motion and hidden pages update immediately, and interruption commits the latest choice. This has transient memory/copy cost, with no physical-phone timing claim.
- **Blue-hour display adaptation.** The authored afterglow/blue-hour fills use neutral blue/slate rather than violet. Atmospheric coefficients remain unchanged. A bounded camera/display green–magenta balance acts in low shadowed sky during deep twilight, carries into horizon haze and the sea's sky reflection, and preserves display luminance, the higher anti-twilight band and the warm toward-sun sector. This is an authored display adaptation, not spectral calibration. The four manual-theme anchors stay the same.
- **Reflections.** The sea mirrors the sky over the hills: at this camera's pitch the sky some 24 degrees up in the foreground and, near the beach, the sky just above the crests. It blends toward those physical colors as the sky shows. The rising sun lays a glitter path across the bay; the setting sun, behind the viewer, lays none. The site mark's orange sun sinking into the Pacific (Sirui, October 26) agrees with this: the footer's sun goes down into that same sea, behind the viewer and out of the frame, while the far right glows toward it.
- **Limits (October 26 PC pass).** The coast remains a stylized miniature. The finer relief and polygon-sized facades improve the distant settlement; they do not turn it into a full terrain/building reconstruction. Most building heights are assumed and small canyons remain soft at footer scale. Some vertical transitions near the Village remain visible. The sea uses a homogeneous, single-scattering approximation; its canyon/reef mesh is authored rather than surveyed, and its caustics and bright-light streaks are approximations. The October 24 costs below describe the earlier renderer under shared load and do not establish the PC pass's current cost. Use the source-bound PC review for current verification and any bounded measurements; the [continuous-dusk review](../artwork/la-jolla/reviews/continuous-dusk-2026-10-26/README.md) records the subsequent page-boundary and blue-hour repairs.
- **The marine layer and its light.** `footer-coast/marine-layer.mjs` lays low fog over the surf line and the beach in three stacked sheets, depth-tested so the coast stands in it. Each sheet reads the sun's shadow map, so the fog stays dim where palms and buildings block the low sun and glows where light passes: at golden hour, shafts reach across the beach toward the viewer. It scatters forward (Henyey-Greenstein), brightest toward the sun, and is densest at dawn and dusk; the theme anchors carry none.
- **Lights coming on.** Windows, the DIB office, the pier lamps and their glitter brighten through the afterglow on the same altitude scale, before the evening fire. Selected panes in selected distant houses glow mostly warm, with a few cooler porches and a much slower, smaller variation; the houses no longer form a near-continuous orange light ribbon. Every selected building remains present. This is an authored pattern, not live occupancy information.
- **A halo for the hour.** The credit's halo (`creditHalo`, CSS custom properties `--coast-halo` and `--coast-glow`) keeps a dense light core beneath dark ink by day and a dense navy core beneath light ink at night. The outer glow follows the hour: warm cream and gold at sunset, rose in the afterglow and cool moonlight at night. The PC pass strengthened the daytime core after the darker water put afternoon credit contrast below 4.5:1 at tablet and phone widths. The credit remains the site's only signature; its rendered contrast gate stays at 4.5:1 around the glyphs.
- **The Connect miniature.** It now runs the same sea on its relief's water slab (`water.mjs` `SEAS`), with its own cove shoreline, a canyon off its pier and reefs off the headland rocks, so it has the clear shallows, surf, wakes, mirrored landmarks and caustics too. `build_la_jolla_miniature.py` was rerun in Blender: its riders are no longer baked, the browser poses them on their boards as on the footer, and the static surf lines gave way to the shader's surf. Its four stills were rendered again.

Reduced motion, Pause motion and offscreen pages keep the sky, the fog and the sea still with the rest of the coast.

## Implementation and budgets

- `_includes/la-jolla-miniature.liquid`, `_includes/la-jolla-footer.liquid`, `_sass/_footer-coast.scss`, and `_includes/footer.liquid` own integration and layout.
- `bin/coastal_life.py` is the single placement source for beach life: courts, the fire circle and logs, benches, parasols, towels, the lifeguard tower, the DIB beach stair and the pier landing, plus every person's seat, path, court or board. It needs no Blender, so `test/test_coastal_life.py` checks props against the terrain, the surf and each other, and confirms that the committed manifest matches it. `build_la_jolla.py` writes its figures into `manifest.json`.
- `assets/js/footer-coast/people-pose.mjs` holds pure posture math: a small two-bone reach for arms and legs, gaits, seated actions, the rally and the dog. `people.mjs` draws every person, prop and contact shadow as five instanced meshes (rods, torsos, ellipsoids, boxes, shadows), so the cast costs a fixed handful of draw calls whatever its size. People outside the camera's horizontal span are not posed, and sitters re-pose every third frame. While animating, the cast costs about 0.4 ms of main-thread time per frame (October 18 CPU profile, 1440-wide footer). A still pose costs only re-placing the riders on their moving boards. `test/coast-people.test.mjs` samples the poses over time.
- `assets/js/footer-coast/entry.mjs` imports the renderer near visibility (450 px), using its content-versioned URL from the page. `assets.mjs` shares decoder, model resources, and geometry; instances have their own materials and light/activity state.
- `scene.mjs` uses the existing pinned Three.js stack, studio reflections, soft sun shadows, bounded contact occlusion, and authored water/plant/character motion. It caps device pixel density at 1.5 and animation at approximately 30 fps.
- `assets/js/footer-coast/water.mjs` draws the sea as one plane and one shader, built from the pure math in `water-optics.mjs` (seabed depth, absorption, refraction, Fresnel, surf phase and breaking, wake angle, mirror points, the on-demand schedule), which `test/coast-water.test.mjs` checks. The waterline reaches the shader as a 1,024-texel strip sampled from the exported profile. Loop bounds are uniforms and hashes are short (lowbias32 over Teschner's spatial-hash primes) because ANGLE's Direct3D compiler unrolls constant-bound loops and inlines every call: the first version compiled in 1.9 s cold against 0.6 s for the old water, the shipped one in about 1.1 times the old. The mirror is a half-float target at about half the canvas density over the water band only; it redraws at most every other frame while the view or the buildings change and not at all in a steady frame. Measured October 18 against main ec8cdb5ac on a bare harness (headless Chromium, ANGLE/D3D11, this laptop's Intel Arc iGPU, medians of five interleaved pairs): at 1440 × 345 the frame stays at the 30 fps cap with 294 draws instead of 291 (the lamp meshes), 2.33 ms of main-thread work per frame against 2.40 and 6.8 ms of GPU time against 6.5; at 390 × 330 (density capped at 1.5) 198 draws instead of 195, 1.86 ms against 1.81 and 6.0 ms of GPU against 5.3, and at night 2.13 ms against 1.95 with 6.3 ms of GPU against 5.6. The first frame draws the mirror once (1,352 draws at 1440 against 1,202), and cold first frames stay level within this shared machine's noise.
- The pier lamps are two instanced meshes (posts with arms, bulbs); the haze is three's linear fog from just past the coast to the hills behind, aimed at a lifted page color because the output pass tone-maps after fog, and at the sky's haze (through the same inverse tone curve) while the sky shows.
- The hills and the sky are one full-screen triangle pair at the far plane, drawn at every hour (by day only the hills show, with the page as their sky). The 1,200 houses are one point draw in front of it, and the marine layer is three transparent sheets that discard outside their band. All of them stay out of the mirror and the contact pass. The houses are not marked `noOcclusion`: the contact pass already hides points and restores them, and the marker would leave them cached hidden. The October 24 sky measured as follows, on this laptop under a full shared load, so with wide spread:
  - Its two programs compile in parallel during the coast's warm-up: a median 410 ms cold against 336 ms for the old sky program (four interleaved pairs, fresh browsers, ANGLE/D3D11, Intel Arc).
  - On the bare harness at 1440 × 345 and sunset, the first frame arrives at a median of 6.8 s against 8.1 s, with main-thread time per animated frame 9.4 ms against 12.3 ms (three interleaved pairs; within noise, no regression seen).
  - The CPU tables take about 9 ms once at mount. Each new sky-view table costs 12 to 45 ms of CPU, and it is built four rows per task between frames, at most once a minute on the clock, and swapped in whole.
  - It adds 13 KB of minified, gzipped script (`atmosphere.mjs`, `skyline.mjs`, `skyline-data.mjs` and the larger `sky.mjs`) and the 10 KB relief image, all loaded with the footer. The footer and the miniature share one sea program: each sea's waterline mapping, canyon and reefs are uniforms. Both scenes compile their programs on the driver's worker threads before the first frame (`renderer.compile` under the finish's half-float target, then readiness polls through `scheduler.postTask`, which a paused test clock does not freeze, bounded at about eight seconds), and nothing draws meanwhile. Measured October 19 against main dcff1a5db on the bare harness (headless Chromium, ANGLE/D3D11, this laptop's Intel Arc iGPU, interleaved pairs, medians; GPU timer queries were unavailable on this run): every steady frame stays at the 30 fps cap; noon draws are unchanged (294 at 1440, 198 at 390) and a sunset adds four (the sky and three fog sheets); main-thread time per frame stays within this shared machine's noise (1440 noon 4.5 against 4.6 ms; 1440 sunset 5.7 against 6.2 ms over five pairs). The miniature draws 75 instead of 84. Cold starts in a fresh GPU process got shorter: the footer at sunset reaches its first frame in 2.3 s instead of 2.7 s with a longest main-thread task of 0.74 s instead of 1.33 s, and the miniature, whose new sea alone had added 0.84 s of blocking compile, in 2.2 s instead of 2.8 s with 0.94 s instead of 1.19 s.
- `coastal_terrain.py` authors the ground at its final width; there is no runtime terrain stretch. The exported shoreline profile drives the water shader as well as the beaches and bluffs. Architecture, paths, veranda, seawall and seals are authored and batched in Blender; the miniature's separate composition is retained.
- `bin/build_la_jolla.py`, `coastal_landmarks.py`, `coastal_campus.py`, `coastal_panorama.py`, `coastal_terrain.py`, and `build_la_jolla_miniature.py` retain editable sources. Repeated authoring primitives reuse finished shapes with independent mesh data before batching, avoiding repeated Blender scene updates.
