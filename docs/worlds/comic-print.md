# The comic print world: rule sheet

A one-page contract to agree before any shader (docs/todo.md, item 12; takeover notes J5 and J8). Written by Claude as creative director on November 17, 2026. Change it when Sirui's eye says so.

## What it is

Sirui, November 13: the _Spider-Verse_ manner, "the new sony bold color", high resolution "yet still micro". On every drawn world: "they should not just cheaply do a trivial shitty transform". The mood for the home: calm and beautiful, never eerie, so the energy here comes from colour and timing, not from noise.

So: the house as a page of a printed comic come alive. Ink lines, bold flat colour printed with dots, the printing's own flaws used on purpose, and motion with the snap of a drawn panel. On the site it is named for how it is made, Comic print; what is taken from the films is technique, never their characters, logos or frames.

## How a comic is printed, and what that asks of the renderer

- **Ink line over flat colour.** A black line drawn by hand: heavier on the outside of a form and on the side away from the light, thinner and broken inside it (the cartoon's structure pass already finds where lines go).
- **Halftone for the mid-tones.** A printed comic makes light and shade from dots of a few inks, larger where darker. Dots carry the mid-tones; hatching carries the darks; the brightest light is bare paper.
- **Misregistration instead of blur.** When the colour plates slip, colour shifts off its line. The films are widely reported to use this in place of a camera's out-of-focus blur: far things print slightly out of register. Read Sony Pictures Imageworks' own accounts before quoting any detail publicly.
- **Bold, complementary colour.** A few strong inks, chosen against each other: warm light against cool shadow, the orange of the site against blue.
- **Its own time.** People move on twos (twelve poses a second) while the camera moves on ones, with holds and snappy overshoot. The house already steps its people this way in drawn looks.

## The rules

1. **Dots and hatching are fixed to surfaces,** scaled so a dot stays about the same size on screen, never sliding over moving things.
2. **Four inks and the paper,** chosen per view from the light, not from the realistic render's colours.
3. **Out of register only with distance:** the subject prints true, the far coast slips a little.
4. **Lines are drawn, not found:** weight follows form and light, and a line can stop short of a corner.
5. **Motion has panels:** holds, then a snap; nothing drifts.
6. **"Micro":** at full resolution the dots and lines are fine and crisp, a print looked at closely, never a coarse filter.

## His figure, a companion, sound

Sirui inked a little bolder than the house, moving on twos. A companion drawn as a comic sidekick with its own small burst of motion lines. Sound: crisp and dry, a page turning between rooms.

## What must not change

The house, its rooms and layout, walking and every control, accessibility and reduced motion (no snapping or flashing then), and the realistic render as the default.

## How it is judged

In three views: the living room by day, the gym mid-lift, and the coast from the sea at dusk. It is ready when a still could be a panel from a well-printed comic, and the motion reads as drawn.
