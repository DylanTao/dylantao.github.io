    Water, air and sky 11
    • A Pacific of Gerstner waves Ten Gerstner waves displace the sea and its normals together, with a wind sea that grows with the breeze; the water’s colour comes from its optics over a seabed read from the coast (turquoise over sand, deep blue offshore, caustics on the sand), with a live mirror that streaks the terrace lamps, the sun’s glints in the hue of the day’s real low sun, and swash on the true waterline. In the scene
    • A spectral ocean, enabled in the lab An actual 64 × 64 inverse FFT drives the sea’s long waves in the authoring lab, sharing the displaced surface with its normals and the coastal animals; the public home keeps the analytic ocean. Exploratory
    • A creek and koi pond on the clifftop A carved recirculating creek descends through six reaches into a koi pond; five original fish swim on the home’s shared active clock. In the scene
    • A height-field onsen The onsen is a 40 × 40 shallow-water height field whose waves carry water between cells, reflect off the rim and the bather, start from the bather’s hand, and throw caustics on the basin. In the scene
    • Thermal steam Bath mist is two transported fields, droplet concentration and temperature, rendered with single scattering that composes in order around the glass and the water. In the scene
    • One wind for the coast One breeze, measured at Scripps Pier and carried by gust fronts that travel downwind, moves the sea, the clifftop planting, the room plants, the curtains, the steam and the particles. In the scene
    • A scattering sky for La Jolla The sky is a scattering atmosphere computed for the actual sun, so the Pacific sunset goes from gold to apricot and rose with the disc sinking, squashed, into the sea at NOAA’s minute, then a pink afterglow, the blue hour, stars on the turning sky and a moon at its true place and phase; the scene’s key light, fill and reflections follow it. In the scene
    • Stars and five bright planets over La Jolla The night sky carries the naked-eye stars of the Yale Bright Star Catalogue where they stand over La Jolla at the minute, only as many as the coast’s suburban glow and the moon leave visible: about four hundred on a moonless night, under a hundred beneath a full moon, coming out one at a time at dusk. Each is a round point about a CSS pixel across, its colour from its B-V index, reddened and twinkling toward the horizon. Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn join the same draw with their own positions and visual magnitudes, without scintillation. In the scene
    • Haze that follows height and the sun The air between the eye and every surface is an exponential haze, dense at the waterline and thin on the bluffs, glowing gold toward a low sun and cool away from it, and the land block, the hinterland and the 400 m sea dissolve into the sky and the open Pacific instead of ending. In the scene
    • Land past the modelled world Past the modelled mainland, hinterland and sea, the dome carries the land on to the horizon: the mesa keeps rising inland, the coast keeps its line north and south, and the camera’s parallax moves the near land against the far as it zooms, where a flat sea-coloured wall with a hard line at the horizon used to stand. Rounded grove silhouettes and meadow patches now vary that distant land, and warm settlement points appear south and inland after dusk while the northern reserve and sea stay dark. In the scene
    • Marine fog, shafts and sunbeams On marine-layer mornings and some evenings a fog bank lies over the water and climbs the cliff, light streams through the shadows of the cliff, the pines and the house in the haze, and at golden hour sunbeams cross the rooms’ dusty air through the glass front. In the scene
