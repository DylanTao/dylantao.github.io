Light paths through the onsen

Experimental · Oct 14, 2026

A plain-JavaScript renderer races path tracing against Metropolis light transport on the sunlight the light well bends through the onsen’s water.

Its own page: Light Paths in the Onsen

How it works, files and sources

createRenderer in core.mjs builds one path sampler that reads every random decision from a fixed coordinate of a sample vector. PathTracer feeds it fresh numbers and MetropolisRenderer feeds it Kelemen and colleagues’ small and large mutations, so the two estimators share every line of path construction. Both use next-event estimation to the sun and sky with power-heuristic multiple importance sampling, after Veach, and water hits use safeguarded Newton iteration on seven frozen ripples. The page runs each estimator in a module worker for the same wall time, and make_light_lab_reference.mjs splits path space at the first hit for a 24-minute reference.

A light well with traced shaft light

Shipped · Oct 14, 2026

A shaft drilled from the clifftop brings sun into the onsen, and its beam, its sun patch, its skylight and the lit steam share one visibility function.

How it works, files and sources

bin/coastal_lightwell.py drills a shaft of 0.42 m radius through 1.67 m of rock, leaning 28° toward the noon sun. createLightWell in light-well.mjs follows four light paths. The sun’s shadow map carries the patch on the floor or the water. Dust and humid air in the beam are single scattering with a forward Henyey–Greenstein phase, marched only inside the shaft’s elliptic cylinder, after Wronski. shaftExitRadiance path-traces the drilled cylinder on a fixed Halton sequence for the radiance leaving the aperture, and the aperture and the sun patch then light the bath as two small area lights in closed form (wellIrradianceGLSL).

Traced sky and lamp bounce

Shipped · Oct 2, 2026

A CPU ray tracer bakes sky visibility and one diffuse bounce into probe cages once, then relights them from the actual sky and lamp powers without tracing again.

How it works, files and sources

createStaticLightField builds a triangle BVH over the house’s static opaque meshes (createTriangleBVH), traces eight cosine-weighted sky rays per primary hit, and stores irradiance as first-order spherical harmonics (accumulateL1, evaluateL1) in 64 probes, eight per room-sized cage, with octahedral distance moments, after Ramamoorthi and Hanrahan and the probe visibility of Majercik and colleagues. Unit sky and lamp bases let a change of hour recombine the cache with zero rays, and since October 19 the three sky colours are fitted to the scattering sky’s irradiance, weighted toward the west the house faces. The field replaces three.js’s ambient terms once; direct light, reflections and refraction stay native. The bake runs in yielded slices and frees its accelerator afterwards. When the renovation moved the rooms, the cages followed: the living room and the gym got cages of their own, 61 of the 64 probes stand in free space, and the living room’s washi lantern, whose closed globe had hidden its bulb from the bake’s 0.18 m emitter radius, declares a 0.26 m emitter so its bounce is no longer zero.

Horizon-based contact light

Shipped · Oct 1, 2026

A visibility buffer computed before the beauty pass darkens only indirect diffuse light near contacts, with the closed-form horizon integral of ground-truth ambient occlusion.

How it works, files and sources

contact-occlusion.mjs projects the geometric normal into four fixed view-aligned slices and integrates the cosine-weighted visibility above each slice’s two horizons in closed form (horizonIntegral), Jimenez and colleagues’ inner integral, with at most 32 depth probes per pixel. A 3 × 3 reconstruction keeps neighbours on the same tangent plane (bilateralWeight), and the paper’s cubic fit adds back the light a pale neighbourhood bounces (bounceVisibility). bindContactLighting scales only each material’s indirect diffuse term, so sunlight, emission and reflections are untouched, and a still composes the same way every time.

Lime plaster, marble and travertine from noise

Shipped · Oct 17, 2026

Walls carry soft clouds and overlapping trowel strokes, the office desk and the kitchen run wandering marble veins, and the tables travertine’s bands and pores, all from noise evaluated at each surface point.

How it works, files and sources

finishPhysicalMaterial keys a procedural kind by material name and builds every pattern from hashed value noise in world-space metres (surfaceNoise), so the scale survives the glTF exports’ many UV layouts. Plaster blends soft clouds about a metre across with streaks of noise stretched along three stroke directions whose mix changes from patch to patch, drawn in the three axis planes and weighted by the surface normal, Geiss’s triplanar texturing, so a curved vault shows no seam; a faint burnish lowers roughness along the strokes. Marble’s veins are the sine of a warped coordinate, the construction of Perlin’s image synthesizer, widened and faded with the pixel’s footprint so they never shimmer; travertine stacks warm bands and small open pores. Each kind perturbs the normal with Mikkelsen’s surface-gradient bump mapping and fades its detail below a pixel.

A Pacific of Gerstner waves

Shipped · Oct 1, 2026

Ten Gerstner waves displace the sea and its normals together, with a wind sea that grows with the breeze; the water’s colour comes from its optics over a seabed read from the coast (turquoise over sand, deep blue offshore, caustics on the sand), with a live mirror that streaks the terrace lamps, the sun’s glints in the hue of the day’s real low sun, and swash on the true waterline.

How it works, files and sources

sampleOcean in ocean-spectrum.mjs sums ten choppy Gerstner components with gravity–capillary dispersion and analytic tangents, after Finch, with footprint filtering after Bruneton, Neyret and Holzschuch; the wind sea, ripples and whitecaps follow the coast’s breeze. Since October 18 createBathymetry reads the seabed from one orthographic draw of the coast, and fillSeabed extends it offshore. The sea shader refracts each view ray to that seabed and evaluates Lee and colleagues’ shallow-water reflectance (shallowReflectance, with seawaterIOPs from Pope and Fry’s pure water), lights the sand with the sun’s shadow and paraxial caustics from the wind ripples (causticGain, the differential form of Watt’s beam areas), breaks the sun’s GGX lobe into glints, draws GGX lamp streaks, and runs the swash on the captured waterline. The glints first took their hue from the slant path’s transmittance (sunTint, after Kasten and Young’s air mass and Hansen and Travis’s Rayleigh depth); since October 19 the water reads the scattering sky’s own sun. createSeaReflection renders the mirror with exact water Fresnel and streak taps.

A spectral ocean, enabled in the lab

Experimental · Oct 23, 2026

An actual 64 × 64 inverse FFT drives the sea’s long waves in the authoring lab, sharing the displaced surface with its normals and the coastal animals; the public home keeps the analytic ocean.

How it works, files and sources

createFFTOcean constructs a seeded Phillips spectrum after Tessendorf, evolves Hermitian Fourier coefficients with deep-water dispersion and runs radix-two inverse transforms across rows and columns. A 128 m periodic tile is normalized to about 0.13 m RMS height at an authored 8 m/s reference wind. One RGBA Float32 texture holds height and central-difference slopes; CPU contacts and GPU sampling use the same periodic four-texel interpolation and mesh origin. The sea lions, sandpipers and pelican water contacts read that field. A separate cache of at most 24 forecast fields supports wildlife plans without replacing the currently rendered texture. Existing bathymetry, shore foam, reflection, wakes and optics stay active. The default analytic path allocates no FFT texture and compiles no FFT branch. The October 23 A/B/B/A measured median frame intervals of 32/34/31/31 ms and submission times of 16/19/15/15 ms on the shared laptop; concurrent work makes these noisy local observations. Texture upload is 65,536 bytes per active frame, with at most 1,572,864 bytes of forecast fields and no new geometry, draw call or reflection pass.

A height-field onsen

Shipped · Oct 2, 2026

The onsen is a 40 × 40 shallow-water height field whose waves carry water between cells, reflect off the rim and the bather, start from the bather’s hand, and throw caustics on the basin.

How it works, files and sources
createHeightfieldWater follows section 2.1 of Chentanez and Müller: staggered heights and face velocities, conservative upwind depth fluxes, pressure from the free-surface gradient and reflecting solid faces, at fixed 1/120 s ticks with CFL subdivision; total volume holds to about 10⁻¹⁴. measurePoolGeometry measures the real basin, about 0.127 m deep. createPoolStroke drives a slow hand skim, and the palm’s submerged velocity relaxes nearby face velocities inside each step, so a resting hand adds no energy. createOnsenWater packs elevation, slopes and wet weight into a texture for the pool’s material and runs only when the camera can see the pool. Since October 18 the basin’s stone takes the water’s light: light from above refracts into the water, and the simulated surface refocuses it by 1/ det(I + l(1 − 1/n) Hess h) (poolCausticGLSL, the differential form of Watt’s beam areas), with six sub-grid capillary-gravity ripples riding on the simulated waves (poolRippleGLSL), so a calm pool stays exactly flat without forcing. Since October 23 a visible drop from the retained bamboo kakei lands in the existing solver as a small zero-mean impulse. Its timing shares the active clock, accumulates no debt while the bath is not stepped, and disposes with the water adapter; the original fixture is unchanged.

Thermal steam

Shipped · Oct 2, 2026

Bath mist is two transported fields, droplet concentration and temperature, rendered with single scattering that composes in order around the glass and the water.

How it works, files and sources

createSteamDensity advects an 18³ concentration and temperature field at a fixed 30 Hz with midpoint semi-Lagrangian backtracing, after Stam, and lets warmer cells rise faster, after Fedkiw, Stam and Jensen’s separate smoke density and temperature; diffusion and cooling use exact exponential factors, and since October 16 the drift follows the house’s share of the coast’s breeze (setWind). createSteamVolume marches up to 40 samples with Beer–Lambert extinction and a Henyey–Greenstein phase (integrateSteamSamples, steamPhase), after Wronski, and splits each ray once at the nearest glass or water (steamRayIntervals) so the gas in front and behind composes in order.

Each is a round point about a CSS pixel across, its colour from its B-V index, reddened and twinkling toward the horizon. Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn join the same draw with their own positions and visual magnitudes, without scintillation.

How it works, files and sources

createStarField in stars.mjs draws one THREE.Points of 3,249 stars over the dome. starMatrix precesses their J2000 positions to the date (IAU 1976, Meeus chapter 21) and turns them by local mean sidereal time (Meeus 12.4); on the live clock they turn with the page clock between minutes. The vertex shader dims each star by the atmosphere’s own transmittance and the coast’s haze along its line of sight, then compares it with the naked eye’s limiting magnitude (limitingMagnitude, after Schaefer) for the sky behind it: twilight from the scattering sky’s table, a 19.4 mag/arcsec² suburban glow that brightens toward the horizon and most toward San Diego, and the moon’s light by Allen’s phase law. Above the limit, brightness rises as the 0.55 power of the flux and size as its 0.14 power, so the brightest stars grow instead of blooming; each point spread is widened by a pixel’s box filter with its light kept, so a moving star neither crawls nor flickers. Colour comes from B-V through Ballesteros’s temperature and the Planckian locus, white-balanced to the 6504 K blackbody (against D65 the locus is faintly magenta between 5,000 and 8,000 K, which had turned white stars pink), and desaturated for faint stars as night vision desaturates them. Scintillation grows as the air mass to the 1.5 power on the scene’s clock and stops under reduced motion; by day the field is not drawn, and the sea mirror leaves it out. It replaced a procedural field of about 100,000 hashed cells whose warm tints read as pink dots. Since October 23 planetPositions solves JPL’s 1800–2050 Keplerian elements, with one light-time iteration, then applies Mallama and Hilton’s phase-dependent visual magnitude equations, including Saturn’s ring inclination. The five points refresh each astronomical minute and use the stars’ existing precession, extinction, horizon hiding, pixel footprint and exposure. Twenty retained seasonal Horizons responses bound sampled error below 0.25° and 0.2 magnitude. The existing moon position and illuminated phase are preserved.

A cached static sun shadow

Shipped · Oct 18, 2026

Casters that never move are drawn into the 2048 px sun shadow map once and copied in each frame; only moving casters are drawn over the copy.

How it works, files and sources

createShadowCache wraps three’s shadow pass. A shared Draco decoder pool

Shipped · Oct 18, 2026

Every model loader on the page shares one pool of up to four Draco decoder workers, so a model’s primitives decode in parallel.

Three’s GLTFLoader decodes each Draco primitive as its own task, and one worker per loader serialized them. createModelLoader gives every loader on the page (the home, its wildlife, the footer coast) one pool of half the cores, at most four, and ends it when the last loader is disposed. In isolation (eight interleaved rounds, warmed decoders, median) the house shell decoded in 118 ms instead of 323, the avatar in 130 instead of 315, the kitchen in 74 instead of 151 and the hinterland in 180 instead of 388. Because the pool outlives any one load, it must not remember a failure: a failed decoder download is fetched again by the next decode, a failed worker is retired, and a retry after a failed load starts fresh.

A drawing-buffer pixel budget with adaptive density

Shipped · Oct 18, 2026

The drawing buffer is capped at 2.1 megapixels, and a full-screen buffer of a megapixel or more steps its density down only while frames wait on the GPU.

How it works, files and sources

Full screen can ask for 2560 × 1440 CSS pixels at a device pixel ratio of 1.5, about 8.3 MP, where the 4× multisampled half-float composer targets alone take on the order of a gigabyte. createResolutionGovernor caps the buffer at PIXEL_BUDGET (a 2560 × 1440 screen at DPR 1.5 draws 1920 × 1080). On buffers of 1 MP or more it steps density down by 0.85, to no less than 0.7, only while frames are slow and waiting on the GPU rather than on script; it keeps a step only if the 75th-percentile frame time improves by 10 %, stops after two unhelpful probes, steps back up after three fast seconds and never moves while the scene is still, after Binks’s dynamic resolution rendering and the hysteresis of drei’s PerformanceMonitor. It scales the logical size handed to the renderer and to the finish composer, never the pixel ratio the composer captured, so every pass stays at the canvas size. In the home’s full screen at 2560 × 1440 and DPR 1.5, main drew 3840 × 2160 at 4.4 fps in the study and 2.7 fps outside; with the budget the buffer was 1642 × 923, at 11.9 and 6.1 fps.

A visibility gate for three’s transmission copy

Retired · Oct 18, 2026

An occlusion-query gate that would have skipped three’s opaque copy for refraction whenever no transmissive surface was visible from a held camera pose.

It was correct but never engaged.   2. Simulation without compute shaders

    Needs a decision Water, air and sky

    The scene renders through WebGL2 and three.js r164, which have no compute shaders. The October 23 opt-in 64² FFT now runs on the CPU with a bounded texture upload; that completes a small spectral experiment without a renderer migration. Larger spectra, a pressure solve for the steam, cloth on the GPU and probe updates would still need fragment-pass chains or WebGPU, a move that touches every material and pass the warmup knows. The performance pass priced that migration on October 18 and deferred it (open problem “When to move to WebGPU”).

    Candidate moves

    • First isolate and time the existing CPU FFT, its texture upload and the analytic default on the same camera before adding GPU complexity.
    • If measured spectrum size or CPU cost warrants it, prototype one FFT fragment-pass chain against that retained CPU implementation.

  3. Light that only a tracer can find

    Needs research Light

    Caustics on the onsen’s vault, glossy reflections of the lit house and light through the sheer all follow paths a rasterizer cannot aim at. Since October 18 the basin’s floor and the sea’s sand take paraxial caustics from their own rippled surfaces, one sheet for light from overhead, but light reflected onto the vault and strongly folded caustics still need a tracer. The light lab finds them on the CPU, but the house has no runtime tracer, and WebGL offers no ray queries.

    Candidate moves

    • Reflect the same paraxial lens off the onsen’s surface onto the vault, checked against the light lab’s reference.
    • Shade the light well’s aperture with glossy area lights, the cheapest specular path the house is missing.

  4. Where the frame still goes

    Needs a decision Performance

    Outside, the frame is still bound by three’s per-draw path. After the October 18 cuts about 800 draws go to the view and three’s transmission copy and about 400 to the sea mirror, and each draw refreshes its own material, about 35 µs of script per draw. The three largest remaining savings change pixels or touch the renovation’s materials, so they need Sirui; the rest are engineering. The moves are in priority order, with the expected win and the effort for one agent estimated from these measurements.

    Candidate moves

    • Share identical materials between meshes, so grouped draws skip three’s material refresh; a further 20 to 40 % of outside script time, in half a day (needs a decision on materials.mjs and realism.mjs).
    • A non-transmissive stand-in for the hopper glass and the distant onsen water, which removes three’s transmission copy outside, about a quarter of the frame’s draws, in hours (a look change of a few dozen pixels).
    • A lighter sea mirror, without interiors or redrawn every other frame while the camera is still; up to half its ~400 draws, in hours (13 of 108 sampled stills changed, median 126 px).
    • BatchedMesh with multi-draw for the hinterland’s 156 static primitives and the planting’s 23, with each piece’s transform baked into its geometry; 100 to 150 fewer draws per pass outside, in a day.
    • Meshopt instead of Draco as a post-process, with quantization matched to the Draco settings and custom attributes kept (the default prune strips the planting’s TEXCOORD_0 and COLOR_1); about 0.3 to 0.5 s less decoding on a first view for about 0.9 MB more, in half a day.
    • Minify the ES modules, which jekyll-terser skips; the 3D modules would go from 330 to 201 KB gzipped and the site’s other 31 from 110 to 72 KB, in hours, keeping bin/profile_coastal_startup.cjs able to find the frame callback.
    • A content-hashed Service Worker cache, because GitHub Pages serves every file with max-age=600 and re-sends about 10 MB of unchanged models after each deploy; a day.
    • Room-level occlusion culling from the coast, by one-frame-late queries with hysteresis, two-phase culling or a CPU depth test against real room hulls; up to 130 draws per pass, in two to three days (pop-in while moving needs a decision).
    • Level of detail for distant planting, simplified chunks with THREE.LOD and then impostors; a large triangle cut at distance, in one to two days and three to five for impostors.
    • Shader compile time at startup (about 70 programs, 12 to 19 s to the first frame on the loaded laptop); measure each program’s compile cost, then fewer variants or explicit-level texture reads in the light field’s probe loops; possibly seconds, after one to two days of investigation.
    • Moving-scene temporal antialiasing instead of 4× multisampled half-float targets, requiring correct motion vectors for skinned, swaying and cloth meshes; the October 23 frozen-photo action is a separate bounded operation and does not complete this method.
    • Cap ambient animation at 60 fps on high-refresh displays, keeping the full rate while the visitor drags; an hour (a smoothness decision).

  5. When to move to WebGPU

    Blocked Performance

    Not now. Three.js r186 (September 24, 2026) still calls WebGPURenderer experimental and recommends WebGLRenderer for pure WebGL 2 applications, and WebGPURenderer runs neither onBeforeCompile patches, ShaderMaterial nor EffectComposer passes. Every string-patched material here (the procedural kinds, the light field, contact lighting, skin diffusion, the light installations, the light well, the plant and ocean displacement) and the contact, bloom and output passes would be rewritten in TSL. Three.js issue 30560, open since February 2025, reports WebGPURenderer’s per-object uniform buffers running several times slower than WebGLRenderer with many distinct objects (about 60 against 15 fps for 20,000 cubes), which is this scene’s profile, and a September 2026 comment there measured its WebGL 2 fallback on r186 at 31 % more render CPU and 80 % more GL calls in a draw-heavy app. WebGPU ships in Chrome on Windows, macOS and ChromeOS since 113, on Android since 121 and on Linux from 144, in Safari 26 and in Firefox 141 on Windows, then on Apple-silicon macOS; everyone else would get the WebGL 2 backend. What it would buy here is render bundles that replay static draws without per-draw script, and compute-driven culling with indirect draws, both aimed at the per-draw cost this scene is bound by.

    Candidate moves

    • Wait until issue 30560 is resolved, so WebGPURenderer is at least level with WebGLRenderer on about a thousand distinct draws.
    • First move the scene from r164 to a current three.js on WebGLRenderer, itself a project, because r182 to r186 rewrote the shadow and BRDF chunks the string patches target.
    • Then port the materials one at a time to node materials and TSL under WebGLRenderer’s NodeMaterial compatibility layer (r184), and switch renderers only if an A/B wins on both the WebGPU and the WebGL 2 backend; several weeks of focused work.
    • Until then, attack the same per-draw cost on WebGL 2 with material sharing, batching and a lighter mirror.
