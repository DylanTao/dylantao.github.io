Graphics Lab

The 3D coastal home on my homepage is where I try graphics and simulation papers: sunlight down a drilled light well, waves that grow with the sea breeze, steam carried by its own heat, curtains that billow at an open door. Other experiments begin with the house itself, like a carved creek feeding a koi pond. This page keeps them in one place, with what each does now and the papers that could take it further. The technical record behind it holds each one's code, sources and limits, and a separate page shows how I keep the house fast.

Read the technical record How I made it fast Open the 3D home
Why

I kept adding graphics experiments to the home, and a project page for each one would have buried the rest of my work.

What

One row per experiment, with a still from the scene, where it stands, the paper it adapts and a link to its write-up.

How

The record distinguishes paper adaptations from original scene work, says where each simplifies, and keeps tests and sources beside the method.

The experiments

Each row opens the experiment’s own page or its section of the technical record. Its caption records the scene’s camera and clock. Most stills date from October 18–19, 2026; the creek and spectral ocean were captured on October 23. The light well, steam and onsen are set in late August, when the noon sun reaches the shaft and the bath runs in daylight. How the house itself is built, from its Blender sources to shader warmup and loading, is in the coastal home’s record. The last group, Performance, opens Making the 3D home fast: where each frame went, what each change saved and what comes next.

The October 23 work adds five bright planets checked against JPL Horizons, a small live cloth around the sleeping duvet’s fitted shape, and the clifftop water garden. The spectral ocean stays separately enabled in the authoring lab; the public home keeps its analytic waves. That lab also has a frozen-photo sampler and a separate static-house ink comparison. Their methods and limits sit beside a review of learned representations, with no trained-pipeline or performance claim borrowed from the papers.

Light

  • Light paths through the onsen

    Lab pageafter Veach and Guibas 1997

    A plain-JavaScript renderer races path tracing against Metropolis light transport on the sunlight the light well bends through the onsen’s water.

  • A light well with traced shaft light

    In the sceneafter Wronski 2014

    A shaft drilled from the clifftop brings sun into the onsen, and its beam, its sun patch, its skylight and the lit steam share one visibility function.

  • Traced sky and lamp bounce

    In the sceneafter Ramamoorthi and Hanrahan 2001

    A CPU ray tracer bakes sky visibility and one diffuse bounce into probe cages once, then relights them from the actual sky and lamp powers without tracing again.

  • Horizon-based contact light

    In the sceneafter Jimenez et al. 2016

    A visibility buffer computed before the beauty pass darkens only indirect diffuse light near contacts, with the closed-form horizon integral of ground-truth ambient occlusion.

Surfaces

  • Lime plaster, marble and travertine from noise

    In the sceneafter Geiss 2007

    Walls carry soft clouds and overlapping trowel strokes, the office desk and the kitchen run wandering marble veins, and the tables travertine’s bands and pores, all from noise evaluated at each surface point.

Water, air and sky

  • A Pacific of Gerstner waves

    In the sceneafter Finch 2004

    Ten Gerstner waves displace the sea and its normals together, with a wind sea that grows with the breeze; the water’s colour comes from its optics over a seabed read from the coast (turquoise over sand, deep blue offshore, caustics on the sand), with a live mirror that streaks the terrace lamps, the sun’s glints in the hue of the day’s real low sun, and swash on the true waterline.

  • A spectral ocean, enabled in the lab

    Exploratoryafter Tessendorf, Simulating Ocean Water

    An actual 64 × 64 inverse FFT drives the sea’s long waves in the authoring lab, sharing the displaced surface with its normals and the coastal animals; the public home keeps the analytic ocean.

  • A creek and koi pond on the clifftop

    In the scene

    A carved recirculating creek descends through six reaches into a koi pond; five original fish swim on the home’s shared active clock.

  • A height-field onsen

    In the sceneafter Chentanez and Müller 2010

    The onsen is a 40 × 40 shallow-water height field whose waves carry water between cells, reflect off the rim and the bather, start from the bather’s hand, and throw caustics on the basin.

  • Thermal steam

    In the sceneafter Stam 1999

    Bath mist is two transported fields, droplet concentration and temperature, rendered with single scattering that composes in order around the glass and the water.

  • One wind for the coast

    In the sceneafter NDBC station LJPC1

    One breeze, measured at Scripps Pier and carried by gust fronts that travel downwind, moves the sea, the clifftop planting, the room plants, the curtains, the steam and the particles.

  • A scattering sky for La Jolla

    In the sceneafter Hillaire 2020

    The sky is a scattering atmosphere computed for the actual sun, so the Pacific sunset goes from gold to apricot and rose with the disc sinking, squashed, into the sea at NOAA’s minute, then a pink afterglow, the blue hour, stars on the turning sky and a moon at its true place and phase; the scene’s key light, fill and reflections follow it.

  • No still yet

    Stars and five bright planets over La Jolla

    In the sceneafter Hoffleit and Warren 1991

    The night sky carries the naked-eye stars of the Yale Bright Star Catalogue where they stand over La Jolla at the minute, only as many as the coast’s suburban glow and the moon leave visible: about four hundred on a moonless night, under a hundred beneath a full moon, coming out one at a time at dusk. Each is a round point about a CSS pixel across, its colour from its B-V index, reddened and twinkling toward the horizon. Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn join the same draw with their own positions and visual magnitudes, without scintillation.

  • Haze that follows height and the sun

    In the sceneafter Wronski 2014

    The air between the eye and every surface is an exponential haze, dense at the waterline and thin on the bluffs, glowing gold toward a low sun and cool away from it, and the land block, the hinterland and the 400 m sea dissolve into the sky and the open Pacific instead of ending.

  • No still yet

    Land past the modelled world

    In the sceneafter Hillaire 2020

    Past the modelled mainland, hinterland and sea, the dome carries the land on to the horizon: the mesa keeps rising inland, the coast keeps its line north and south, and the camera’s parallax moves the near land against the far as it zooms, where a flat sea-coloured wall with a hard line at the horizon used to stand. Rounded grove silhouettes and meadow patches now vary that distant land, and warm settlement points appear south and inland after dusk while the northern reserve and sea stay dark.

  • Marine fog, shafts and sunbeams

    In the sceneafter Wronski 2014

    On marine-layer mornings and some evenings a fog bank lies over the water and climbs the cliff, light streams through the shadows of the cliff, the pines and the house in the haze, and at golden hour sunbeams cross the rooms’ dusty air through the glass front.

Cloth, skin and motion

  • Linen curtains and laundry

    In the sceneafter Macklin, Müller and Chentanez 2016

    Four sheer linen panels on rings and bronze rods, and three towels and a shirt on a terrace line, in one small-step XPBD cloth with isometric bending, blowing in the coast’s one wind.

  • A fitted duvet with live cloth constraints

    In the sceneafter Müller et al. 2007

    Each avatar’s duvet is fitted over the sleeping body offline, then a small compliant cloth follows the breathing chest in the scene while preserving that authored contact envelope.

  • Preintegrated skin diffusion

    In the sceneafter Christensen and Burley 2015

    Skin spreads direct light across a curved patch with a normalized diffusion profile, so a shadow’s edge on the face turns warm instead of hard.

  • Strand highlights on hair

    In the sceneafter Kajiya and Kay 1989

    Hair gets two shifted highlight bands across its strands, the primary and secondary lobes of hair scattering, instead of a round spot.

  • Lifts posed on each figure

    In the sceneafter Tolani, Goswami and Badler 2000

    Every lift in Sirui’s program is solved each frame on the avatar’s own skeleton, so the hands meet the real bar whatever the body’s proportions.

  • Animals that plant their feet

    In the scene

    Twenty-eight skinned animals, from rabbits and sea lions to brown pelicans and Brandt’s cormorants, act in irregular epochs sampled from time, with feet that hold their contacts on the exported terrain, rock and sea.

  • Gliders soaring the bluffs

    In the sceneafter the banked-turn relation

    Three paragliders and a hang glider follow closed loops along the north bluffs, banking into their turns, posed from the scene clock.

Camera and land

  • A camera that knows the house’s walls

    In the sceneafter Amanatides and Woo 1987

    Every static mesh is voxelized into a 12 cm field once it loads, and a spring arm keeps the camera on the near side of anything between it and the subject, so visitors can orbit freely and zoom the coast to 160 m.

  • The land beyond the mainland’s walls

    In the sceneafter Shewchuk’s Triangle

    A terraced mesa with badland canyons, bluffs with the gliderport, a headland with sea caves and a distant point continue the coast past the mainland’s flat walls.

Drawing

  • A pen sketch while the house loads

    In the sceneafter Saito and Takahashi 1990

    A pen draws the house from its own traced outlines, silhouettes and creases while it loads, gated by finished work and densest just before the first frame.

Next papers to try

Each technique in the record keeps a short list of newer work that advances it, mostly from SIGGRAPH, SIGGRAPH Asia, Eurographics, EGSR and SCA, with what it would add here and a rough effort. These four would each change something a visitor can see:

  1. Light paths through the onsen · Zeltner, Georgiev and Jakob 2020, SIGGRAPH 2020

    Medium effort Solves for the path through the water from a point on the pool floor to the sun, so even plain path tracing could aim at the sun. The lab’s pool has a single refracting surface, the method’s simplest case.

  2. A Pacific of Gerstner waves · Jeschke et al. 2020, SCA 2020

    Medium effort Keeps procedural waves but makes them respect the terrain’s boundary; with the captured seabed the swells could slow, shorten and turn toward the beach.

  3. A height-field onsen · Jeschke and Wojtan 2023, SIGGRAPH 2023

    Medium effort Splits each step into shallow-water bulk flow and Airy surface waves, so the hand’s short ripples would travel at their true speed in this shallow basin.

  4. One wind for the coast · Diener et al. 2009, Eurographics 2009

    Medium effort Projects the wind onto a few precomputed modal stresses per tree, so the Torrey pines and the palm would sway as whole structures, evaluated on the GPU.

How the stills and the record are made
  • Stills. bin/capture_graphics_lab_stills.cjs opens the homepage's authoring lab in headless Chromium at a named hour, room and camera, without the homepage's feathered edge, and bin/make_graphics_lab_thumbnails.py scales the frames. The October 23 creek and FFT frames come from bin/verify_environment.cjs against the candidate scene. They are scaled, never retouched. The light lab's still is a frame of its own renderer.
  • Record. Each technique lives in _data/design_records/graphics-lab.yml with its files and key functions, its sources, its limits and its papers to try; python bin/design_record_brief.py graphics-lab turns it into a brief for the next pass, and the agent brief says how to take a paper from the list into the house.
