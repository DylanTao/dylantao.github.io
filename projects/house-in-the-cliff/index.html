The House in the Cliff

The house in my homepage's 3D world began as a room on a rock. It now sits eight metres inside a sandstone cliff above the Pacific, on two floors, with a library, a pool court and separate ways in. Each change below started with a render that looked wrong, to me or to the agents building it.

Back to the build diary Read how it is built
Why

A room on a pedestal reads as a stage set. I wanted a home you could believe was cut into a La Jolla cliff.

What

Two floors carved from one continuous cliff, a glass front, a library and a pool court cut into the rock.

How

Before and after views, measured contacts on the reviewed routes, and another look at the result.

How the house grew

These are the house’s entries from the build diary, with the details and extra views that did not fit there. June–July

The first room

A 3D view joins the paper desk, then becomes one room seen from inside and out.

  1. Jul 14 Day 28 House

    One room, seen from inside and out

    My note

    The first 3D version copied the desk into a small stage. At ordinary screen sizes, furniture disappeared, dropped cards floated, and the outside looked like a separate dollhouse.

    What changed

    The desk stage became a room in the cliff, with an onsen, a lounge chair and an ocean window, and inside and outside became two camera views of that one room. The 2D desk's record and discoveries carry into it.

September

Moving in

September's reviews turned the room into a two-floor home in a sandstone cliff.

  1. Sep 13 Day 89 House

    The cliff turns to sandstone

    My note · reviewing commit 564ca01fe

    Build the Pacific, the cliffs and the outside as real geometry, never a picture behind the room, and keep only the Realistic look in public.

    What changed

    The public 3D view keeps one Realistic look. The cliff has continuous fluting and erosion instead of detached relief chips, and sky, haze, moving water and a shoreline wash are drawn in the scene. No scenic image sits behind the home.

    Architectural and Illustrated stayed behind as experiments in the authoring lab, with a note on what a future attempt would have to change in form, materials and composition before they come back.

    Review notes Commit 788508a82

  2. Sep 13 Day 89 House

    Two floors carved into one cliff

    My note · the left image is my own review screenshot

    A room on a freestanding pedestal reads as a stage, not a home in a cliff. Carve the cave into land that continues inland, with the beach at its foot and the ocean beyond.

    What changed

    The study, sleeping alcove and onsen moved to an upper gallery 2.6 m above the kitchen, lounge and gym, joined by fifteen closed risers. The cave is carved from one continuous mainland, the beach follows the shore beneath it, and the cutaway lifts only the local ceiling.

    Hiding the whole roof had erased the mountain behind the rooms, which is why only the local ceiling lifts. Walking began to follow the exported stair treads instead of an approximate equation.

    Review notes Commit 5cd5432fc

October 24

A long day of fixes

The house moves eight metres into the rock, and the plants, the first-person view and the desk's coffee ring get their fixes.

  1. Oct 24 Day 130 House

    The house steps eight metres into the cliff

    My note

    The house belongs inside the cliff. The pool should read as a light court on real rock, with a dry walk from the living room, and upstairs should be a quiet private floor.

    What changed

    Only the house moved, eight metres landward; the coast stayed where it was. The pool now sits in a basin cut into the rock, without the deep girder and three exposed piers. My desk moved into a tall library, and the old upstairs office became a quiet room with two chaises.

    The setback has a cost I kept visible. From the pool, the cliff faces now enter its sideward ocean view, and the playful laundry line still crosses that camera. The excavated sandstone is still angular, and the bedroom is still a long walk from the library.

    Built with Codex Review notes Commit 162685ac8

  2. Oct 24 Day 130 House

    Plants placed on purpose

    My note

    I wanted the view to lead, the plants to feel deliberately placed, the bathroom finishes to feel considered, and the library to have a warm hearth.

    What changed

    The freestanding olive left the doorway to the glass front. A stone bed at the north window now holds a fan of Strelitzia, small ferns sit on the vanity stone, and a pothos trails from the library shelves. The library gained a recessed stone hearth.

    The bathrooms gained a narrow limestone border, larger stone cuts in the dry zone and finer ones in the shower, and a concealed light under each vanity. These renders are Blender previews of the source, not the browser, so they show placement and form rather than the page's night lighting.

What is still open

The October 24–26 walk-through is still guiding the work. Three problems from the retreat remain open because they need new geometry and another review:

  • The bedroom is still a long walk from the library, across a five-metre bridge. A stair beside the library needs its own chamber, new floor openings and fresh checks that people and P can still get through.
  • The excavated sandstone is angular. Matching its colour to the cliff does not make it look eroded. Softer cliff transitions mean real new geometry that still holds up the pool and the terrace.
  • The laundry line still crosses the pool view. Moving it means moving its posts, its cloth and the wind that moves it together, and it stays where it is until a matched view shows the move is better.
How these comparisons were made
  • Browser frames. Each caption names its view and capture conditions. The retreat's camera moves eight metres with the house, so those views stand side by side rather than pretending to line up pixel for pixel.
  • Blender previews. The plants and the bathroom are Eevee previews of the source file in the same room camera, before and after. They show placement and form, not the browser's night lighting.
  • Review screenshots. The September pedestal image is my own screenshot from a review, so it is not the camera of the frame beside it.
  • Custody. Entries link their retained review notes and commits. This house page draws its images from docs/evidence/ and artwork/coastal-home/reviews/. In the full diary, the coffee-ring and Connect originals were laptop-local; their WebP derivatives are retained here.
