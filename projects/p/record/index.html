duh & P, a technical record

How the site’s two companions are built. duh is the soft black dot that lives on every human page: a stable center with a deformable outline that you can pet, drag and throw. P is the little robot that came first; it keeps its motion playground on the project page and its articulated model in the 3D home. Both are original browser characters that move with authored motion, not a physics engine or a language model.

Neither character uses a camera, a microphone, audio or a remote model, and neither is an assistant. Their remarks are local strings. Pointer proximity is the only visitor signal, and listening is a visual pose. No visitor study has been run, so nothing here claims that they improve engagement.

duh’s body

duh is a stable center collider wrapped in a deformable outline. The physics, the silhouette, the mood and the shape repertoire are separate, so each can change without breaking the others.

A center collider with explicit gesture states

Shipped · Oct 8, 2026

duh’s center moves on fixed 1/120 s steps with a velocity ceiling and damped wall contacts, and its gestures run through six named states.

How it works, files and sources

duh-motion.mjs integrates the center at fixed 1/120 s steps, at most six from one 50 ms input frame, under a 1,600 px/s ceiling, with damped contacts against the viewport walls. Contacts with text lines are swept, a moving circle against rectangles, so a fast throw cannot skip a thin paragraph, and a surface the body already overlaps at release is ignored. Throw velocity comes from recent timestamped release samples, and a stationary hold discards old velocity. The states are REST, PLAY, HELD, AIRBORNE, TIDY and RETREAT. There is no queued animation, a new drag interrupts motion at once, and cancellation never becomes a throw. On phones the throw speed limit scales down with the screen width.

An inertial shell around the center

Shipped · Oct 8, 2026

A bounded secondary shell adds gravity sag, grip lag, direction-aware stretch and damped shaking without moving the collider.

How it works, files and sources

duh-body.mjs drives the shell from the grip’s acceleration, with substeps that bound even a delayed input frame. Gravity pools the base on a surface, and a held shell hangs below the grip. The contour stays area-normalized, with bounded radial dents. Position-based dynamics is background reading for controlled deformation here; the shell does not implement that solver.

Shapes, eyes and theme-keyed shading

Shipped · Oct 8, 2026

One 96 × 96 canvas draws duh from twenty fixed-angle radii that morph between a dot, an apple, a peach, a watermelon, a rounded square and a triangle.

How it works, files and sources

duh-portrait.mjs joins twenty radii with quadratic midpoints that stay inside their control hull, at a device-pixel ratio of at most 2, and morphs between shapes with an analytic damped radial spring that cannot drift with the frame rate. Volume comes without per-pixel normals: a rim band away from the key light, a key highlight that rides the deformed outline, and a core shadow toward the contact, all keyed to the four time-of-day themes. Fruit stipple and rind stripes are static, not animated noise. The eyes lead an excursion, widen in flight, ease into giggles and close for sleep, and the contact shadow uses the deformed outline.

A soft-body preview

Retired · Oct 13, 2026

An XPBD ring with stretch, bending, area and shape-matching constraints, previewed behind ?duh=soft next to the classic body.

What it was and why it was retired

The ring was attached the way duh is actually held: a grip arc while carried, static friction at the base on a surface, and a centroid anchor only in flight. That gave about 8 px of swing under a 15,000 px/s² shake, 4.5 px of lean from a push, a resting pool 1.1 times as wide as tall, and under 0.5 % volume error. Activity counted velocity, not deformation, so a pooled shape stopped keeping the page drawing at 60 fps.

Why it was retired
Side by side with the inertial shell, its motion read as jittery and its physics as broken. Its theme-keyed rim, key and core shading moved to the classic body, and the preview’s code and tests were removed. 0102b70c3

duh on the page

duh shares the reading surface with the words. It perches only in clear space, relocates with a visible jump, and any contact with the page is a reversible illusion that never touches the text itself.

Perches in clear space

Shipped · Oct 8, 2026

duh rests and travels only where it covers no reading text, link, control, canvas or protected figure.

How it works, files and sources

duh-page.mjs uses P’s clearAt helper and chooseDuhPerch over cached obstacle rectangles: prose, headings, definition lists, captions, project fact strips, links, controls, canvases and anything marked data-duh-protect. Style and class changes on the main column or its ancestors invalidate the cache even when the content keeps its size, and a rescan keeps the current position and any still-clear path. P’s moving playground character is not treated as a wall. duh-viewport.mjs finds short gaps that the inherited row grid misses between a phone’s reading block and the coastal footer.

Relocation as a jump

Shipped · Oct 13, 2026

When duh has to move off text, it jumps there with anticipation instead of fading and sliding.

How it works, files and sources

A necessary relocation, such as after landing beside text, is a jump: a 0.1 s wind-up, an arc 28 to 90 px high sized to the distance, over whatever is in the way, and a landing squash at the perch already found clear. Travel duration follows distance, and a minimum-jerk curve gives anticipation, acceleration and a soft landing. The separate contact shadow grows and fades with height over its landing plane. Anticipation and arcs are two of the principles Lasseter carried from hand-drawn animation into computer animation.

Reversible contact with the page

Shipped · Oct 8, 2026

A strong throw can nudge nearby lines and rock the play corner’s block; everything settles back and duh makes a small repair.

How it works, files and sources

Swept contacts use at most 160 cached surfaces, including the ink-line bounds of short paragraphs, headings and list items. A strong hit moves text by at most 4 px and 0.35°, an off-center hit on a heavy object by at most 3 px with up to 1.8° of rock, and at most three nodes react, with a damped oscillation and a small residue for duh to repair. The motion is an additive compositor transform: text is never split or reparented, and selection clears the effect immediately. A pointer press on a link freezes its visual position until the click is dispatched, with the animation’s hold time pinned synchronously, so the native action keeps its target. The play corner’s four decorative words get pointer-transparent copies that fly and come home.

Pets, throws, rests and small adventures

Shipped · Oct 8, 2026

A released click pets duh, rough throws earn a short breather, and in empty space it sometimes hops, rolls or turns into fruit.

How it works, files and sources

Three close clicks give one happy squish, and clicks never count as roughness. Three intentional releases above 850 px/s within 12 seconds arm an eight-second breather after duh settles; collisions never count. A retreat is a small tucked pose at the departure point, and tapping it brings duh back early. An approach needs a stationary fine pointer in empty nearby space, a clear path and a 12-second cooldown, and the anticipated hop stops 43 px from the pointer. Spontaneous hops, rolls and shape changes wait while a visitor types, selects or plays.

Input, accessibility and stillness

Both companions are optional company on a reading page. They answer keyboard, mouse and touch, stay out of scrolling and selection, and keep a composed still state when motion is reduced or paused.

duh as a semantic button

Shipped · Oct 10, 2026

The character itself is the control. Tap or Enter pets or wakes it, keys move and toss it, and Alt+Shift+D recalls it from anywhere.

How it works, files and sources

A stationary 700 ms hold pauses, Shift-click or R resets in place, P pauses, H greets, T tosses and the arrows move duh. Its native tooltip, accessible description and the project page explain the actions, and its label stays in sync when pausing, waking or resetting. An earlier fixed corner panel and separate Pause and Reset buttons were removed, so pause and reset live on the character. A graphics failure keeps the button and a simple dot. Single-pointer alternatives to dragging follow WCAG’s guidance on dragging movements.

Touch without stealing the page

Shipped · Oct 9, 2026

Only the 56-pixel character takes over touch; the rest of the page scrolls and selects as usual, even while browser bars resize the viewport.

How it works, files and sources

Only the character button uses touch-action: none and disables callouts and selection. Pointer capture survives viewport updates, coalesced samples keep the hand’s motion, duplicate samples are ignored, and the release point completes the velocity estimate. Coordinates use the layout viewport; VisualViewport offsets, visible size and safe-area insets define the usable bounds, so a browser-bar resize updates the bounds without canceling the gesture. Cancellation, a second pointer, blur or leaving the page clears the gesture without a throw. Double-tap followed by a tap on clear space moves duh without dragging, and never consumes a link’s native action.

Stillness on request

Shipped · Sep 13, 2026

Under reduced motion or the site’s Pause motion, both companions keep their shapes and expressions but stop pursuing, bouncing and shaking.

How it works, files and sources

duh switches shapes immediately and holds static expressions, with no pursuit, bounce, shake, scattered pieces or animated cleanup, and paused motion accumulates no physics. P composes a still greeting pose and its brief rests end automatically. Pause motion in the theme panel makes the reduced-motion media query match for every listener, so both characters still through the same path as the system preference. Hidden pages stop drawing, and frame intervals are capped rather than replaying background-tab time.

P’s model and portrait

P exists in three renderings that share one motion controller: an articulated Blender model in the 3D home, a small analytic WebGL portrait on reading pages, and an enlarged playground on its project page.

An articulated Blender model

Shipped · Sep 14, 2026

A rounded ceramic head, a tapered shell, two convex lenses, two antennae and detached arms, each on its own pivot, exported as one GLB.

How it works, files and sources

bin/build_pip.py authors P in Blender 4.5.9 LTS through its background Python API, with a studio camera and lights, and exports named head, antenna, eye and arm pivots to assets/models/pip/pip.glb (848,688 bytes, 27 meshes). The browser poses those pivots at runtime; there is no skeletal clip or physics. The optical catchlights follow the pupil positions, matching the portrait. A transparent Cycles render becomes the poster and the fallback when graphics fail. The design draws on Reachy Mini’s expressive head and unequal lens eyes and on EVE’s floating tapered body; the geometry is original.

P’s motion and attention

One pose controller drives every rendering of P. Gaze leads, the head follows, the body takes its time and the antennae settle last; in the room, a small director stages a visitor encounter.

One pose controller with minimum-jerk timing

Shipped · Sep 14, 2026

Original gestures blend with pointer attention through standard minimum-jerk transitions and damped antenna springs.

How it works, files and sources

motion.mjs owns the hello, curious, nod, peek, repair and sleepy poses, and later listen, stretch and delight. Each transition follows the standard zero-velocity, zero-acceleration quintic, the minimum-jerk trajectory of Flash and Hogan, and an interrupted gesture starts from its current pose. Eyes and neck follow an exact cascaded low-pass response in closed form, so the eyes arrive first at any frame rate; filled pupils widen, squint and wink independently, and fins wave, startle and tuck in. Choreography follows the real frame interval down to 4 fps, with substeps that keep the antenna springs stable. Reachy Mini’s movement API informed the separation of head, antennae and body targets; no SDK or recorded motion is bundled.

A visitor encounter in the room

Shipped · Oct 2, 2026

In the 3D home, P notices a nearby pointer, greets once, listens, then returns to Sirui’s activity or the record.

How it works, files and sources

performance.mjs directs the encounter. A nearby pointer triggers one greeting; staying nearby cannot repeat it, and a new encounter needs an absence and an 18-second cooldown. Eyes lead the neck, the shell follows later and the antennae settle on their own, after the habituation and layered gaze in Pan and colleagues’ robot gaze work. attention.mjs resolves targets in P’s own optical frame, so orbiting the room never makes it look at the wrong side of an object, and accepts a genuine touch tap as an invitation while rejecting pans, cancellations and multiple fingers. Stillness ends an encounter but keeps its cooldown, so resuming does not wave again.

P on the page and in the room

On reading pages P plans routes around the actual rendered rectangles; in the room it flies its own measured corridors between safe perches. One owner decides where P lives at any moment.

Routes around rendered text

Shipped · Sep 14, 2026

P flies around prose, figures, links and controls, squeezes through gaps that fit, and opens a pair of portals when a crossing is blocked.

How it works, files and sources

travel.mjs builds a visibility graph over the expanded corners of live obstacle rectangles, capped to the viewport, and rounds the path only where the whole curve has clearance. One arc-length clock carries momentum through bends instead of restarting the easing at every corner, and P looks toward its route during a short departure pause. A squeeze shrinks in place and folds the flippers before a gap that fits the smaller footprint; a blocked crossing uses two portals at clear endpoints, and P moves only while invisible. Scrolling, resizing, pause, reduced motion, hiding and navigation cancel travel and remove the portals.

Rare bumps with a repair

Shipped · Sep 14, 2026

Most encounters are avoidance. A rare nudge to a card or heading has a visible approach, a small reversible wobble and a repair gesture.

How it works, files and sources

After an initial 55 to 105 seconds, automatic bump attempts come every 85 to 150 seconds with a 30 % chance, and proximity or clearance can still suppress them. P never nudges a hovered or focused element, and pause, reduced motion, hiding or navigation cancel the transform. Ordinary remarks wait about 45 to 80 seconds between attempts and disappear after 3.5 seconds.

One owner across page, room and playground

Shipped · Sep 13, 2026

Exactly one renderer owns P at a time, and handing it over neither duplicates its mood nor fast-forwards a flight.

How it works, files and sources

bridge.mjs holds the single companion state that moves between the reading surface, the 3D room and the playground. When the room loses ownership, its director is interrupted without touching the motor the page now drives; regaining it composes a quiet task behind the arrival fade. Active time, route progress and cooldown stay fixed while the page owns P. Since October 8 ordinary pages mount duh, so P’s room adapter stays dormant outside its tests and the project page.

Flights along measured air corridors

Shipped · Oct 2, 2026

In the room P rises before it moves, cruises 1.65 m above its route, rounds corners inside a 12 cm envelope and settles at locally fitted perches.

How it works, files and sources

clearance.mjs gives P its own corridor around shelf contents and the coat by the stair, measured against the shell’s 26 cm reach below its origin; the human circulation points stay untouched. performance.mjs times each flight with a minimum-jerk distance curve, banks within 0.16 rad, keeps peak speed under 1.6 m/s and finishes with under a centimetre of settling. Room changes queue P’s latest destination until it has physically arrived, because starting a new route mid-flight reset the floor and could jump P 2.6 m.

Design history

Each move follows the loop from Donald Schön's The Reflective Practitioner: frame the problem, make a move, let the result talk back, then reframe. The dimensions are the variables the move changed. Oldest first; new moves are added at the end.

Open problems

  1. Are the interruptions welcome?

    Needs research duh on the page

    No visitor study has been run. Occasional hops, remarks and bumps may charm in a short visit and grate in a long reading session, and the thresholds that decide how often they happen are authored guesses.

    Candidate moves

    • A small session study with a reading task, comparing duh present, paused and absent, and counting interruptions noticed and dismissed.

  2. Cheaper layout scans

    Measure first duh on the page

    duh re-measures its reading obstacles and up to 160 cached contact surfaces whenever layout invalidates. Work that stalls a two-core test runner also stalls a slow phone, which is why the October efficiency work starts with these rescans.

    Candidate moves

    • Profile rescans per invalidation type on a phone-class CPU.
    • Rebuild only the rectangles inside changed subtrees instead of the whole cache.

  3. A real touch drag on an iPhone

    Ready Input, accessibility and stillness

    WebKit’s test driver supplies native taps and pointer capture but no native touch drag, so drag, throw and double-tap-move have only been checked in emulation and Chromium touch injection.

    Candidate moves

    • A recorded manual pass on a physical iPhone, including a browser-bar resize mid-drag.

  4. A soft body that does not read as broken

    Needs a decision duh’s body

    The XPBD preview was numerically stable and still read as jittery next to the inertial shell. Any return has to beat the shell in a matched recording, at an idle cost of nearly nothing.

    Candidate moves

    • Use a soft solver only for one held moment, such as a squeeze, and the shell everywhere else.

    Read

    • Macklin, Müller and Chentanez 2016
    • Müller et al. 2005

  5. duh inside the 3D home

    Needs a decision P on the page and in the room

    duh stays on the page in both homepage modes, and the room’s companion slot holds P’s dormant adapter. A 3D duh needs a body model, contacts with the room and an ownership handoff like P’s.

    Candidate moves

    • Reuse P’s ownership bridge and perches for a 3D duh, starting with one room.

Keep intact

  • duh is the only default companion on human pages, in both homepage modes. P stays on its project page, and one owner moves P between the page, the room and the playground.
  • AI routes stay undecorated and motion-free.
  • No camera, microphone, audio, remote model calls or proprietary character assets. Remarks are local, authored strings.
  • Real text, links, selection and page order stay intact. Only the play corner’s decorative words become loose copies, and scrolling, selecting, pausing or resetting restores everything at once. Nothing persists across a refresh.
  • Reading clearance protects prose, figures, links, controls, canvases and anything marked data-duh-protect.
  • Reduced motion and Pause motion keep the shapes and expressions, with no pursuit, bounce, shake or scattered pieces.
  • A relocation needs a visible cause, such as a jump; never a half-transparent slide.
  • Hidden pages stop drawing, and the idle scheduler samples at one hertz.
  • Keep P’s two antennae, unequal convex lenses, white tapered shell, orange dot and detached fins recognizable, and keep the Reachy Mini, dance library and EVE credits visible.
