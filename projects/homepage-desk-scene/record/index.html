Two views, one desk

One state across 2D and 3D

Shipped · Jun 17, 2026

The 2D 3D switch changes the view, not the state, so the selected record, its spin, the discovered cards and the avatar carry across.
How it works, files and sources

assets/js/home.js owns the 2D desk and the shared record state. Choosing 3D lazy-loads home-scene/controller.mjs through a small adapter, so the default page never downloads Three.js or the house. The choice is kept in sessionStorage for the session. If WebGL or a model fails, the controller dispatches home-scene-unavailable and the page returns to the 2D desk with its state intact. A cached back/forward return pauses and restores the controllers instead of rebuilding them.

A driven record player

Shipped · Oct 1, 2026

The platter is a driven rotor and the tonearm two critically damped coordinates, shared by the 2D player, its CSS fallback and the 3D study platter.

How it works, files and sources

record-motion.mjs targets 33⅓ RPM with a first-order motor, ω(t+dt) = ω_target + (ω − ω_target)·e^(−dt/τ), and integrates the angle exactly, so the result does not depend on frame rate. The motor responds in 0.72 s and brakes in 0.30 s. Each tonearm coordinate follows a critically damped equation with an exact update; the arm must lift clear of the disc before it swings, and a one-sided contact keeps the stylus above the record. All time is active time, so hiding the player adds no impulse. The inertia, 0.0036 kg·m², is illustrative rather than measured.

One active clock

Shipped · Sep 14, 2026

Walks, clips, water, steam, particles and wildlife all advance on active elapsed time, never on the frame count.

How it works, files and sources

The controller’s clock stops while the scene is paused, hidden, offscreen or under reduced motion, and its timestamp resets on recovery. Solvers take fixed ticks and accept at most 0.25 s per call; anything beyond that is reported as dropped time rather than replayed, so a stalled tab never returns with a catch-up queue. Route and clip timing follow active time, so a software renderer with seconds between frames does not stretch a walk. Animal acting and the record are sampled from absolute active time, so they cannot drift.

Loading and the first frame

What happens between choosing 3D and the first composed view. Models stream in a fixed order, shader programs compile without freezing the page, a loading bar reports only finished work, and a pen sketch fills the wait.

Streaming order and Draco decoding

Shipped · Sep 12, 2026

The shell, the coast, the occupied room and the selected avatar load first; the other rooms and the clifftop planting stream afterwards.

How it works, files and sources

Startup never draws a partial house while the occupied room or the avatar is still loading. The models are Draco-compressed GLBs exported from the Blender sources. model-loader.mjs decodes them through a pinned decoder served as ordinary same-origin files, because the loader’s default inline blob worker conflicts with the site’s Content-Security-Policy. Since October 18 every loader on the page (the home, its wildlife, the footer coast) shares one pool of up to four decoder workers, which decodes a model’s primitives in parallel: the house shell in 118 ms instead of 323 when measured in isolation. Streamed rooms join the house only once their shader programs are ready, and the clifftop planting (flora.glb, 2.2 MB) loads the first time a visitor goes outside.

Shader programs compiled before the first draw

Shipped · Oct 12, 2026

Every program the first view needs is created ahead of the draw and compiled on the driver’s worker threads, in small batches, so the page keeps scrolling while the house compiles.

How it works, files and sources

Three.js compiles a program the first time it draws a material, and the draw blocks until the driver finishes. On ANGLE/D3D11 each of this scene’s programs took 0.1 to 1.8 s, and the first view froze the page for about 13 s. program-warmup.mjs reproduces each draw’s compile state exactly (target, lights, fog, object, shadow side), so the proxy it compiles is the program the real draw later finds. With KHR_parallel_shader_compile it asks whether a program is done instead of waiting. It creates programs in batches of six materials with a task boundary between them, and submits the next batch only while no more than half the logical cores’ worth of programs are compiling. Polling uses the browser scheduler because test clocks freeze timers. Software rasterizers skip the warmup.

A loading bar that reports finished work

Shipped · Oct 13, 2026

A pen sketch while the house loads

Shipped · Oct 14, 2026

Pen strokes draw the sea with today’s sun or moon, rise into the cliff, open into the cut-away house and settle into the occupied room, then trace the first rendered frame as it fades in.

How it works, files and sources

loading-sketch.mjs hands an OffscreenCanvas to a worker so model parsing never stalls the strokes, and draws on the main thread where a worker cannot. The drawings are traced offline by bin/build_loading_sketch.cjs from object-ID and surface-normal passes of the authored views: object outlines weighted by how much of the frame each object fills, creases sharper than about 57°, and long straight runs favored by the structure tensor’s coherence, after Saito and Takahashi’s line drawings from geometry buffers. Eight drawings pack into 45 KB at three bytes a stroke. Strokes fly on a damped spring (ζ 0.68) along slight arcs, stretch along their velocity, pair with their next targets in Hilbert-curve order so a morph flows instead of tangling, and boil on twos once they land. The render is never held back more than 1.4 s. Reduced motion shows each drawing still, and automation sees the plain loading state unless the URL carries ?cinematic=live.

Camera envelopes from the finished terrain

Shipped · Sep 14, 2026

How it works, files and sources

Semantic zoom and panning

Shipped · Oct 14, 2026

Zooming changes what is shown. Pushing past a room’s widest view opens the whole house, then the coast, and zooming in walks back into the room under the pointer.

How it works, files and sources

A view changes only after a further push within 0.7 s (the zoom factors’ logarithms summing past 0.32), so one hard flick still stops at the edge. Shift-drag, right- or middle-drag, two fingers and Shift+arrows move the viewpoint across the floor, within 1 m of a room’s authored target, 6 m of the house’s and 26 m outside. In the whole house, hovering names a room and a click or the keys 1 to 6 visit it. ⌘/Ctrl+scroll, which a trackpad pinch sends, zooms without focusing the canvas; a plain scroll still scrolls the page and shows a hint at most three times. The idea is Perlin and Fox’s Pad and Bederson and Hollan’s Pad++, with the home’s own hierarchy (room, house, coast) as the zoom axis.

A collision field voxelized from the loaded house

Shipped · Oct 15, 2026

Every static house, room and nearby coast mesh is voxelized into a 12 cm field once it loads, so the camera knows the house’s real walls, furniture and rock.

How it works, files and sources

createCameraField in camera-field.mjs voxelizes each mesh in a module worker (camera-field-worker.mjs), or in small background slices where workers are unavailable, and camera.mjs classifies the cells. Connected pieces under 45 cm in every direction are detail and block only in a dense canopy; a structural cell blocks when its 7-cell neighbourhood holds a plane’s worth of structure (walls, floors, cabinets, beds, rock, dense slat screens), while rails, posts, mullions and the rack’s uprights stay thin. Blocking cells grow a 24 cm clearance shell, and air is flooded from each room’s target and authored camera, so the inside of a solid is never a place to stand. Glass, the cut-away roof, sliding doors and props are left out; the onsen steam is an explicit solid. Rays walk the field cell by cell, after Amanatides and Woo.

Spring arm, free orbits and steering

Shipped · Oct 15, 2026

The camera sits at the farthest point on its orbit ray that the path reaches without crossing a blocking cell, so rooms open to free orbits and pans that slide along obstacles.

How it works, files and sources

The arm is the farthest point on the orbit ray the path reaches without crossing a blocking cell, with eight side rays on a 7° cone pulling in before an edge reaches the ray. It shortens within about 0.1 s, recovers over about 0.4 s and never passes a hard limit 10 cm before the first blocking surface. Orbit, pan and coasting steps that would leave the arm under 40 % of the requested distance are refused, sliding along obstacles. Once the field is ready, rooms get free yaw, pitch from −0.12 to 1.25 rad and zoom to 1.5 times the authored distance, and room pans reach 1.8 m and stop at furniture. Released drags coast with a 0.32 s decay in closed form; reduced motion and Pause keep drags exact; Home recentres and Page Up and Page Down zoom.

Full screen with a way back

Shipped · Oct 18, 2026

A Full screen button beside Look around, or F on the scene, fills the screen with the stage; Escape, a Back to the site pill or leaving 3D returns the page where it was.

How it works, files and sources

createWorldFullscreen shows the scene edge to edge without the page’s vignette, floats the caption bar at the bottom as frosted glass and puts a Back to the site pill top left, with an Esc hint on fine pointers. Browsers with the Fullscreen API get real full screen (requestFullscreen({ navigationUI: "hide" })). iPhone Safari, which has no element full screen, gets the same layout as a manual popover in the top layer, so the page’s own stacking and transforms cannot clip it, plus a pushed history entry, so the back gesture closes it instead of leaving the site. While open, the stage is a labelled modal dialog and focus stays inside. The existing ResizeObserver resizes the renderer, and the pixel budget keeps a large screen within 2.1 megapixels.

Horizon-based contact light

Shipped · Oct 1, 2026

A visibility buffer computed before the beauty pass darkens only indirect diffuse light near contacts, instead of multiplying the finished image.

How it works, files and sources

contact-occlusion.mjs projects the geometric normal into four fixed view-aligned slices and integrates the cosine-weighted visibility above each slice’s two horizons in closed form, Jimenez and colleagues’ inner integral, with at most 32 depth probes per pixel within 0.36 m. A 3 × 3 reconstruction keeps neighbors on the same tangent plane and rejects separate depth layers and creases. The paper’s cubic fit adds back the light a pale neighborhood would bounce, so linen and stone corners do not turn grey. The result scales only indirectDiffuse inside each material; direct sun, emission and reflections are untouched. One wind for the coast

Shipped · Oct 16, 2026

One wind state drives the sea, the planting, the room plants, the curtains, the steam and the particles, so a gust that roughens the water reaches the bluff seconds later and the curtains when it meets the glass.

How it works, files and sources

wind-field.mjs runs a periodic cubic Hermite through the October averages at Scripps Pier (NDBC LJPC1, 2023–2025): a land breeze from the east at night, a 0.7 m/s lull at 09:00 while the wind turns through north, a west-north-westerly sea breeze peaking at 2.75 m/s at 15:00, and a lighter evening. Gusts are a function of retarded time t − x·d/c, with c the mean speed (Taylor’s frozen turbulence). They come in hashed 8-second slots, each rising over 1.2 s and decaying over 3.5 s, in patches 70 m across. The envelope averages 1, with a turbulence intensity of 0.15 and a ten-minute gust factor of 1.67. The lowbias32 integer hash is exact in JavaScript and in GLSL ES 3.00, so the GPU matches the CPU to 3.3 × 10⁻⁵. The sea turns and grows its wind waves (a Pierson–Moskowitz cut, never above the authored heights), scales its pixel ripples by the Cox–Munk slope law in the local, gusty wind, and adds Monahan whitecaps. Indoors the breeze leaks in at 1.2 % with the doors shut and 6 % with them open. The spray rides the open wind, and the dust and steam drift in the house’s air. coastWind.vane(x, z) serves a wind sock.

One path sampler, two estimators

Experimental · Oct 14, 2026

A dependency-free JavaScript renderer draws a simplified onsen with path tracing and with primary-sample-space Metropolis light transport from the same path sampler.

How it works, files and sources

assets/js/light-lab/core.mjs traces a box grotto, a round pool with seven frozen analytic ripples and a radial drip ripple, a disc light well and a 0.8° sun. Every random decision reads a fixed coordinate of a sampler. Path tracing feeds it fresh numbers; Kelemen and colleagues’ Metropolis feeds it mutated ones, evaluated lazily with PBRT’s bookkeeping. Both use next-event estimation to the sun and sky with power-heuristic multiple importance sampling, after Veach. Water hits use safeguarded Newton iteration and a certified march that cannot skip a crossing, and hot loops write into typed arrays so tracing allocates nothing.

A race with the same minute each

Experimental · Oct 14, 2026

The lab page runs the two renderers in module workers for the same wall time and charts their error against a converged reference.

How it works, files and sources

page.mjs starts one worker per renderer only on request, pauses after a minute, off screen and in hidden tabs, and shows still frames before a run and where a browser cannot run one. Error is the luminance RMSE on an 80 × 60 grid divided by the reference mean. After a minute in the recorded browser run, path tracing was off by 984 % and Metropolis by 180 %; across three seeds Metropolis ended 4 to 15 times closer.

Open problems

  1. A spectral ocean

    Needs research Water and atmosphere

    The Pacific is ten authored Gerstner components. A spectral FFT ocean would give realistic wave statistics, but WebGL2 has no compute shaders, so the transform needs several ping-pong passes, cascades to hide tiling, and a CPU sampler that agrees with it, because floating objects and arrival rays read the same wave heights.

    Candidate moves

    • Prototype a 128² FFT height field on a separate page and compare it with the authored spectrum from the same camera.
    • Keep the authored components for CPU sampling and use the FFT only for normal detail, so contacts stay exact.

  2. Caustics on the onsen vault, and beyond one sheet

    Needs research The light-paths lab

    Since October 18 the basin’s stone takes paraxial caustics from the simulated surface (the onsen-water technique), one sheet for light from overhead. Light that reflects off the rippled water onto the vault, light from the low pool light, and strongly folded caustics are chains of specular bounces that must end in a small sun or a small lamp. Next-event estimation cannot aim through the water, which is the case the light lab measured, and the house has no runtime ray tracer.

    Candidate moves

    • Add the pool light to the lab scene and render its caustics on the vault.
    • Reflect the same paraxial lens off the surface onto the vault, and check it against the lab’s reference.
    • Try manifold exploration or specular manifold sampling in the lab before anything reaches the house.

  3. The outside view on integrated GPUs

    Needs a decision Rendering and light

    Outside, about 1,600 draws a frame went through four passes: the view, the shadow map, the opaque copy refraction needs, and the sea mirror. The October 18 performance pass cut script time per outside frame from 89.8 to 56.9 ms and draws to about 1,290 without changing a still beyond a few tie pixels (the graphics record’s performance system), but the frame is still bound by three’s per-draw work: about 800 draws for the view and the opaque copy, about 400 for the mirror, each refreshing its own material. Most objects are interactive, animated, wind-swayed or skinned, so merging meshes stays off the table, and every remaining large saving changes pixels.

    Candidate moves

    • A distant, non-refracting onsen material and hopper glass outside, which drops the opaque copy, about a quarter of the frame’s draws.
    • A lighter sea mirror, without interiors or redrawn every other frame while the camera is still (13 of 108 sampled stills changed).
    • Share identical materials between meshes, so grouped draws skip three’s material refresh.
    • Skip objects smaller than a pixel (about 180 draws).
    • Judge each with a matched A/B before shipping.

  4. Temporal antialiasing

    Blocked Rendering and light

    Without per-object motion vectors, temporal antialiasing would trade a little shimmer for ghosting on the characters, animals, plants and waves, which move on almost every frame. 8× multisampling cost about 3 ms of GPU time for no visible gain.

    Candidate moves

    • Motion vectors for skinned and wind-deformed meshes.
    • A still photo mode where a temporal filter can accumulate.
    • Revisit under WebGPURenderer’s temporal reprojection node once the graphics record’s WebGPU conditions hold.

  5. Light that sees people and plants

    Needs research Rendering and light

    The traced light field is static and has one bounce, so the avatar, P and the swaying plants never shade it. Retracing on the CPU every frame is too slow, and probe updates in the style of DDGI rely on GPU ray tracing that WebGL does not offer.

    Candidate moves

    • Subtract soft analytic occluders (capsules) for the avatar and P from probe visibility.
