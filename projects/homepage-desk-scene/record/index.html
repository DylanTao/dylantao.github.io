Two views, one desk

One state across 2D and 3D

Shipped · Jun 17, 2026

The 2D 3D switch changes the view, not the state, so the selected record, its spin, the discovered cards and the avatar carry across.
How it works, files and sources

assets/js/home.js owns the 2D desk and the shared record state. Choosing 3D lazy-loads home-scene/controller.mjs through a small adapter, so the default page never downloads Three.js or the house. A cached back/forward return pauses and restores the controllers instead of rebuilding them.

A driven record player

Shipped · Oct 1, 2026

The platter is a driven rotor and the tonearm two critically damped coordinates, shared by the 2D player, its CSS fallback and the 3D study platter.

How it works, files and sources

record-motion.mjs targets 33⅓ RPM with a first-order motor, ω(t+dt) = ω_target + (ω − ω_target)·e^(−dt/τ), and integrates the angle exactly, so the result does not depend on frame rate. The motor responds in 0.72 s and brakes in 0.30 s. Each tonearm coordinate follows a critically damped equation with an exact update; the arm must lift clear of the disc before it swings, and a one-sided contact keeps the stylus above the record. All time is active time, so hiding the player adds no impulse. The inertia, 0.0036 kg·m², is illustrative rather than measured.

One active clock

Shipped · Sep 14, 2026

Walks, clips, water, steam, particles and wildlife all advance on active elapsed time, never on the frame count.

How it works, files and sources

The controller’s clock stops while the scene is paused, hidden, offscreen or under reduced motion, and its timestamp resets on recovery. Solvers take fixed ticks and accept at most 0.25 s per call; anything beyond that is reported as dropped time rather than replayed, so a stalled tab never returns with a catch-up queue. Route and clip timing follow active time, so a software renderer with seconds between frames does not stretch a walk. Animal acting and the record are sampled from absolute active time, so they cannot drift.

Streaming order and Draco decoding

Shipped · Sep 12, 2026

The shell, the coast, the occupied room and the selected avatar load first; the other rooms and the clifftop planting stream afterwards.

How it works, files and sources

Startup never draws a partial house while the occupied room or the avatar is still loading. The models are Draco-compressed GLBs exported from the Blender sources. model-loader.mjs decodes them through a pinned decoder served as ordinary same-origin files, because the loader’s default inline blob worker conflicts with the site’s Content-Security-Policy. Streamed rooms join the house only once their shader programs are ready, and the clifftop planting (flora.glb, 2.2 MB) loads the first time a visitor goes outside.

Shader programs compiled before the first draw

Shipped · Oct 12, 2026

Every program the first view needs is created ahead of the draw and compiled on the driver’s worker threads, in small batches, so the page keeps scrolling while the house compiles.

How it works, files and sources

Three.js compiles a program the first time it draws a material, and the draw blocks until the driver finishes. On ANGLE/D3D11 each of this scene’s programs took 0.1 to 1.8 s, and the first view froze the page for about 13 s. program-warmup.mjs reproduces each draw’s compile state exactly (target, lights, fog, object, shadow side), so the proxy it compiles is the program the real draw later finds. With KHR_parallel_shader_compile it asks whether a program is done instead of waiting. It creates programs in batches of six materials with a task boundary between them, and submits the next batch only while no more than half the logical cores’ worth of programs are compiling. Polling uses the browser scheduler because test clocks freeze timers. Software rasterizers skip the warmup.

A loading bar that reports finished work

Shipped · Oct 13, 2026

A five-segment progress bar fills only from real events, so it can never run ahead of the work or move backward.

How it works, files and sources

world_controls.liquid renders a role="progressbar" with five segments: the house, the coast, the room, Sirui, and the view’s programs. controller.mjs fills the first four from each model’s streamed bytes and parse, and the last from submitted warmup batches and the programs the driver reports finished. A pen sketch while the house loads

Shipped · Oct 14, 2026

Pen strokes draw the sea with today’s sun or moon, rise into the cliff, open into the cut-away house and settle into the occupied room, then trace the first rendered frame as it fades in.

How it works, files and sources

loading-sketch.mjs hands an OffscreenCanvas to a worker so model parsing never stalls the strokes, and draws on the main thread where a worker cannot. The drawings are traced offline by bin/build_loading_sketch.cjs from object-ID and surface-normal passes of the authored views: object outlines weighted by how much of the frame each object fills, creases sharper than about 57°, and long straight runs favored by the structure tensor’s coherence, after Saito and Takahashi’s line drawings from geometry buffers. Eight drawings pack into 45 KB at three bytes a stroke. Strokes fly on a damped spring (ζ 0.68) along slight arcs, stretch along their velocity, pair with their next targets in Hilbert-curve order so a morph flows instead of tangling, and boil on twos once they land. The render is never held back more than 1.4 s. Reduced motion shows each drawing still, and automation sees the plain loading state unless the URL carries ?cinematic=live.

Camera envelopes from the finished terrain

Semantic zoom and panning

Shipped · Oct 14, 2026

Zooming changes what is shown. Pushing past a room’s widest view opens the whole house, then the coast, and zooming in walks back into the room under the pointer.

How it works, files and sources

A view changes only after a further push within 0.7 s (the zoom factors’ logarithms summing past 0.32), so one hard flick still stops at the edge. Shift-drag, right- or middle-drag, two fingers and Shift+arrows move the viewpoint across the floor, within 1 m of a room’s authored target, 6 m of the house’s and 26 m outside. In the whole house, hovering names a room and a click or the keys 1 to 6 visit it. ⌘/Ctrl+scroll, which a trackpad pinch sends, zooms without focusing the canvas; a plain scroll still scrolls the page and shows a hint at most three times. The idea is Perlin and Fox’s Pad and Bederson and Hollan’s Pad++, with the home’s own hierarchy (room, house, coast) as the zoom axis.

Baked contact and bounce light

Shipped · Sep 14, 2026

Horizon-based contact light

Shipped · Oct 1, 2026

A visibility buffer computed before the beauty pass darkens only indirect diffuse light near contacts, instead of multiplying the finished image.

How it works, files and sources

contact-occlusion.mjs projects the geometric normal into four fixed view-aligned slices and integrates the cosine-weighted visibility above each slice’s two horizons in closed form, Jimenez and colleagues’ inner integral, with at most 32 depth probes per pixel within 0.36 m. A 3 × 3 reconstruction keeps neighbors on the same tangent plane and rejects separate depth layers and creases. The paper’s cubic fit adds back the light a pale neighborhood would bounce, so linen and stone corners do not turn grey. The result scales only indirectDiffuse inside each material; direct sun, emission and reflections are untouched. There is no random rotation or temporal history, so a still composes identically every time.

Traced sky and lamp bounce

Shipped · Oct 2, 2026

A CPU ray tracer bakes static sky visibility and one diffuse bounce into seven probe cages once, then relights them from the actual sky and lamp powers without tracing again.

How it works, files and sources

static-light-field.mjs builds a balanced triangle BVH (light-trace-core.mjs) over the house’s static opaque meshes, traces eight cosine-weighted sky rays per primary hit, and stores irradiance as first-order spherical harmonics in 56 probes with octahedral distance moments, after Ramamoorthi and Hanrahan and the probe visibility of Majercik and colleagues’ DDGI. Unit sky and lamp bases let a time-of-day change recombine the cache with zero rays (0.27 ms in the CPU proof). The field replaces Three’s diffuse hemisphere and environment irradiance once; direct light, PMREM reflections and refraction stay native. The bake runs in yielded slices (3.67 s with an 18.7 ms longest task in the CPU proof) and releases its roughly 37 MB accelerator afterwards.

Preintegrated skin diffusion

Shipped · Oct 2, 2026

Skin redistributes direct diffuse light across a curved patch with a normalized diffusion profile, so a terminator turns warm instead of hard.

How it works, files and sources

skin-diffusion-profile.mjs integrates Christensen and Burley’s normalized two-exponential profile over a locally spherical patch with 96-node Gauss–Legendre quadrature and stores the result in a 257 × 49 half-float table (about 100 KB) per finish. skin-diffusion.mjs measures mean curvature in meters from view-space derivatives of the skinned position and normal, then replaces only the direct diffuse term of Three’s RE_Direct_Physical with a 70/30 mix of diffused and ordinary response. The default distances, 3.2, 1.2 and 0.65 mm for red, green and blue, are authored values, not a measured fit to anyone’s skin. Specular, sheen, hair, glasses and clothes keep their shaders.

Anti-reflective glass front

Shipped · Oct 14, 2026

The cave front and the terrace balustrade use low-iron, anti-reflective glass, about 1 % reflectance head-on, so the cut-away house stays readable by day.

How it works, files and sources

glazing.mjs draws each pane over what is behind it with premultiplied blending: its color is only what it reflects, and the view through is dimmed by that share plus a little absorption. A Schlick term still turns the pane into sky toward grazing angles, as real glass does. The panes are not transmissive, because a transmission material would refract a copy of the opaque scene and lose the transparent onsen water and steam behind the front. Glass casts no shadow and does not occlude the contact pass, the volume depth or the light field; its frames do.

A light well with traced shaft light

How it works, files and sources

One path sampler, two estimators

Experimental · Oct 14, 2026

A dependency-free JavaScript renderer draws a simplified onsen with path tracing and with primary-sample-space Metropolis light transport from the same path sampler.

How it works, files and sources

assets/js/light-lab/core.mjs traces a box grotto, a round pool with seven frozen analytic ripples and a radial drip ripple, a disc light well and a 0.8° sun. Every random decision reads a fixed coordinate of a sampler. Path tracing feeds it fresh numbers; Kelemen and colleagues’ Metropolis feeds it mutated ones, evaluated lazily with PBRT’s bookkeeping. Both use next-event estimation to the sun and sky with power-heuristic multiple importance sampling, after Veach. Water hits use safeguarded Newton iteration and a certified march that cannot skip a crossing, and hot loops write into typed arrays so tracing allocates nothing.

Open problems

  1. A spectral ocean

    Needs research Water and atmosphere

    The Pacific is ten authored Gerstner components. A spectral FFT ocean would give realistic wave statistics, but WebGL2 has no compute shaders, so the transform needs several ping-pong passes, cascades to hide tiling, and a CPU sampler that agrees with it, because floating objects and arrival rays read the same wave heights.

    Candidate moves

    • Prototype a 128² FFT height field on a separate page and compare it with the authored spectrum from the same camera.
    • Keep the authored components for CPU sampling and use the FFT only for normal detail, so contacts stay exact.

    Read

    • Tessendorf, Simulating Ocean Water
    • Algis et al. 2025
    • Bruneton, Neyret and Holzschuch 2010

  2. Caustics on the onsen vault and floor

    Needs research The light-paths lab

    Light reaching the vault or the pool floor through rippled water is a chain of specular bounces that must end in a small sun or a small pool light. Next-event estimation cannot aim through the water, which is the case the light lab measured, and the house has no runtime ray tracer.

    Candidate moves

    • Add the pool light to the lab scene and render its caustics on the vault.
    • Precompute a caustic pattern from the lab and project it in the onsen, animated by the solver’s slopes.
    • Try manifold exploration or specular manifold sampling in the lab before anything reaches the house.

    Read

    • Veach and Guibas 1997
    • Jakob and Marschner 2012
    • Zeltner, Georgiev and Jakob 2020

  3. The outside view on integrated GPUs

    Needs a decision Rendering and light

    Outside, about 1,600 draws a frame go through four passes: the view, the shadow map, the opaque copy refraction needs, and the sea mirror. Most objects are interactive, animated, wind-swayed or skinned, so batching could save 3 draws. Every remaining saving changes pixels.

    Candidate moves

    • A distant, non-refracting onsen material outside, which drops the opaque copy (about 400 draws).
    • Skip objects smaller than a pixel (about 180 draws).
    • Judge each with a matched A/B before shipping.

    Read

    • Amdahl 1967

  4. Temporal antialiasing

    Blocked Rendering and light

    Without per-object motion vectors, temporal antialiasing would trade a little shimmer for ghosting on the characters, animals, plants and waves, which move on almost every frame. 8× multisampling cost about 3 ms of GPU time for no visible gain.

    Candidate moves

    • Motion vectors for skinned and wind-deformed meshes.
    • A still photo mode where a temporal filter can accumulate.

  5. One wind for the whole coast

    Ready Wind and plants

    The room plants, particles and steam share the curl field, but the clifftop planting sways on its own sine rule and its shadows see the rest shape. Unifying them means patching the planting’s beauty and depth materials with the same field and response, without breaking the divergence guarantee or the shader cache keys the warmup relies on.

    Candidate moves

    • Bind the clifftop materials to coastalWind with the damped response from plant-motion.mjs.
    • Give the planting a custom depth material that runs the same bend.

    Read

    • Bridson, Hourihan and Nordenstam 2007

  6. Cloth that moves

    Needs research Characters and animation

    The duvets are offline drapes and nothing in the scene is runtime cloth. A curtain in the breeze or a duvet that breathes needs a solver that stays stable within the frame budget, attached to animated bodies.

    Candidate moves

    • Start from the colored Gauss–Seidel solver in bin/coastal_bedding.py and port a small extended position-based version to the browser for one curtain.
    • Drive it with the shared wind field.

    Read

    • Müller et al. 2007
    • Fratarcangeli, Tibaldo and Pellacini 2016
    • Macklin, Müller and Chentanez 2016

  7. Light that sees people and plants

    Needs research Rendering and light

    The traced light field is static and has one bounce, so the avatar, P and the swaying plants never shade it. Retracing on the CPU every frame is too slow, and probe updates in the style of DDGI rely on GPU ray tracing that WebGL does not offer.

    Candidate moves

    • Subtract soft analytic occluders (capsules) for the avatar and P from probe visibility.

    Read

    • Majercik et al. 2019

  8. Grotto or open onsen

    Needs a decision Rendering and light

    The light well makes the bath a grotto that gets sun only through the shaft, so outside midday it is darker than the open cutaway was. Both are defensible; it is a taste decision.

    Candidate moves

    • Keep the grotto.
    • Open the onsen and show the well only from the clifftop; ?onsen-light=open previews it.

  9. A sunset hour on the terrace

    Needs a decision Characters and animation

    The terrace has loungers, a bench and a fire bowl but no activity in the routine. A new activity needs fitted contacts, a camera that looks through a door opening, and a place in the schedule.

    Candidate moves

    • Add an evening terrace activity with contacts on a lounger or the bench, timed with the festoon and fire bowl.

  10. Architectural and Illustrated treatments

    Blocked Rendering and light

    In September the three treatments read as too similar, and Realistic worked best. A shader swap alone does not make a different way of seeing the same home.

    Candidate moves

    • A same-camera comparison where each treatment has its own form, light, mark-making and motion, kept behind ?scene-lab=1.

  11. Close-up character and environment detail

    Needs research Characters and animation

    The scene is a stylized browser world. Film-studio character surfacing and close-up environmental detail remain a higher target, bounded by download size and frame budget.

    Candidate moves

    • Pick one close-up (a face at the desk, the onsen rim) and compare a detail pass against the current frame at the same camera.
