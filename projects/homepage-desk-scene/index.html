The Desk That Learned Depth

The homepage desk is one set of objects in two views. The 2D collage starts with my portrait, papers, and records; 3D opens an inhabited coastal home. Five cartoon versions of me share its daily rhythm, while the music and discoveries stay with you between views.

Why

I wanted the paper desk to feel like a place without making the quick 2D homepage slower to read.

What

A quick 2D desk and an inhabited home with ten connected spaces, sharing the same records, papers, and discoveries.

Try

Switch views, play a record, find a hidden card, and check that it is still there when you switch back.

The build diary

A building crew keeps a site diary: dated photos, the client’s notes, and what changed. This is mine for the 3D home. I look at the rendered place and write down what looks wrong; AI coding agents change the geometry, light and code; then I look again. When the agents’ own review caught a problem first, the entry says so. Each entry keeps one note and the evidence for what changed. Matched views have a divider you can drag or move with the slider; different cameras stand side by side and say why. Day 1 is June 17, when the 3D view first shipped. The notes draw from the retained reviews and commit records.

October 1–12

Details that hold up close

Fingers, lamps and loading get the attention a close look asks for.

  1. Oct 1 Day 107 People

    Fingers that hold the bar

    The review found

    In the gym, the Ghibli hands stayed open above the pull-up handles, and the handle passed through the wrist.

    What changed

    Twenty finger joints joined the Ghibli rig, and both grip clips were fitted to the wrist positions beside the bars. In 132 left/right hand samples across the two clips, every digit had source vertices within 2 mm of its bar. Those samples do not prove continuous collision clearance.

    Review notes Commit 0fc3b00ef

  2. Oct 2 Day 108 House

    A warmer home after dark

    The review found

    At night every room was lit evenly, the lamps were weakly placed, and the bath shirt read as skin.

    What changed

    A light field traced on the CPU now carries sky and lamp light through the house with one bounce, and four warm fixtures light it at night. The bath shirt became opaque teal fabric.

  3. Oct 12 Day 118 Speed

    Moving shader work off the page

    The review found

    Opening the 3D home froze the page for about 13 seconds, then again when the rooms streamed in and on the first Look around.

    What changed

    The October 12 checkpoint prepared the first view's shader programs before drawing and waited for parallel driver compilation. On the tested Windows desktop with an RTX 3080 Ti, blocking program-log reads across the session fell from 26.0 to 0.6 seconds. Two fixed study and exterior views were pixel-identical. The house has grown since, and loading remains an open task.

September: a home with a daily rhythm

The room now has a study, kitchen, gym, onsen, ocean terrace, and sleeping alcove. An authored San Diego routine moves me between them: breakfast comes late, coding continues into the night, and the onsen faces the Pacific. It is a little imagined day, not a live location feed.

I kept 2D as the first view on every screen. In 3D, the occupied room follows the routine; Look around takes you out to the coast. I am refining the Realistic treatment, with stone, warm wood, and moving water. Architectural and Illustrated are deferred experiments in the authoring lab. The cliff and ocean remain actual parts of the world when you look around.

The first character pass did not look enough like me. Reviewing it against my portrait led to an exposed forehead, swept-back long hair, wire glasses, and a broader Lizard jaw. Likeness and the small gestures remain things to judge in the rendered scene, not just in a concept board.

A small floating companion, P, now wanders between the room and the reading page. P means Prototype, with a nod to ProtoLab. Its project page has a motion playground and credits for the robot references that shaped it.

Why the desk has two views

I liked how quickly the 2D collage introduced the papers, portrait, and records, but I also wanted visitors to wander through that little world. The first 3D version copied the desk into a small stage. At ordinary screen sizes, furniture disappeared, dropped cards floated, rear turns exposed blank space, and the outside looked like a separate dollhouse.

The fix was not more decoration. I made inside and outside two camera views of the same room, then shared the record player and discoveries with the 2D desk.

Two eras, four frames

The four frames hold everything I could keep constant: the same route, 1440 × 1000 viewport, light theme, Yellow Submarine stopped, and an empty discovery pile. Each pair starts in 2D and changes only the view. The June pair is an exact-commit replay; the July pair is the accepted checkpoint.

The July decisions

Kept

The visible 2D | 3D switch, shared music and discoveries, paper-and-record identity, and clear ways to look outside and return.

Cut

The shallow stage, hand-matched exterior miniature, floating-card recovery, and camera framing that hid the furniture.

Next

Keep the room shape, then test the stone, lighting, materials, and controls on more phones and high-DPI laptops.
